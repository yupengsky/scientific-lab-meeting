## Scientific question

Across matched task families, model scales, and context lengths, are induction-head-like paths necessary for task-level ICL, or can distributed/staged paths support the same behavior when those heads are removed?

---

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

Whether causal evidence from small attention-only models generalizes to broad task ICL in larger language models, and whether induction heads are a core mechanism or one component of a composite mechanism.

---

## Established evidence

- Small-model ablations and formation interventions causally affect a macro loss-based ICL measure (Olsson 2022; Singh 2024).
- Semantic induction-head analyses in open LMs are mainly attributional or correlational, while other work localizes task inference, verbalization, and intermediate states (Ren 2024; Tao 2024; Yang 2025).

---

## Relevant explanations

### H1
Induction-head-like paths are necessary for task-level ICL across the tested scale and task regimes.

### H2
Induction-head-like paths contribute to some regimes, while distributed or staged paths can preserve task-level ICL after their targeted removal.

---

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

Both allow induction heads to matter in restricted settings; they differ on necessity for broad task-level ICL.

---

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Existing causal tests do not jointly match task type, metric, model scale, and intervention strength. Head ablation may be confounded by redundancy, compensation, or a mismatch between macro loss and task learning.

---

## Potential discriminating observation

Matched head/path interventions produce different residual task-learning performance across small and large models, with task-mapping and retrieval controls. H1 predicts a substantial, reproducible loss of task-level ICL after induction-path removal; H2 predicts preserved performance in at least some regimes with compensatory distributed-path activity.

---

## Provisional attack

Use model-matched activation patching, targeted head/path ablations, and recovery tests across synthetic mapping, retrieval-controlled, and natural-language classification prompts, measuring both behavior and task-state readouts.

---

## Why now

Available induction-head formation methods, causal component interventions, and task-state/answer-state probes make a matched cross-scale comparison more tractable than earlier correlational extrapolations.

---

## Scope

Attention-based transformer language models spanning small controlled models and selected open LMs; synthetic arbitrary-label mapping, retrieval-controlled classification, and bounded natural-language classification. It does not claim coverage of all generation or regression.

---

## Negative-result information

If H1 fails, the result would constrain induction-head claims to particular tasks or metrics and establish that preserved ICL can arise through other paths. If H2 fails, broad causal generalization of induction mechanisms would gain support within the tested scope.
