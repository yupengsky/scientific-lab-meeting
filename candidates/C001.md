# Candidate C001

## Source uncertainty

**Problem-map node:** U02

## Scientific question

In tight-clearance insertion, does an explicitly estimated contact state improve corrective control over a history-matched implicit recurrent policy because it supplies identifiable information about relative pose and contact mode?

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

Current task success does not show whether tactile/force histories identify actionable contact state, or whether policies succeed through task-specific correlations without an identifiable state estimate.

## Established evidence

Contact-map matching localizes pose in restricted simulations; recurrent and tactile/vision policies complete physical insertion in their respective narrow regimes. Modality comparisons remain architecture and training-confounded. [U02; chhatpar_branicky_2003; inoue_2017; dong_2021; lenz_2024]

## Relevant explanations

### H1

Sensor histories contain an identifiable contact-state signal; an explicit estimator improves correction and transfer when the controller receives the same observations and action history.

### H2

The observed histories need not identify a transferable contact state; apparent policy performance arises from task-specific sensor-action correlations, so explicit estimation does not improve matched control or transfer.

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Existing studies do not pair independent ground-truth relative pose/contact-mode measurements with matched explicit-estimator and implicit-policy controllers under common geometry and uncertainty distributions.

## Potential discriminating observation

With the same compliant action space, training data, sensing history, and compute budget, compare an explicit state-estimator controller and a recurrent controller while independently measuring relative pose and contact mode. H1 predicts estimator accuracy should track correction and retain benefit under held-out geometry/error regimes; H2 predicts no such linked, transferable advantage.

## Provisional attack

Use a rigid insertion family with controlled translational/angular error, clearance, and friction. Instrument relative pose and contact mode independently; report estimator accuracy, correction trajectory, force/safety outcomes, seating, and recovery. Pre-register matched observation/action interfaces and held-out geometry and error strata.

## Why now

Open tactile/vision/force papers and contemporary state-estimation approaches make common-interface comparisons technically feasible, while the current literature still conflates sensing, representation, policy architecture, and training.

## Scope

Rigid, fixtured, tight-clearance insertion. The result would not establish performance for deformable parts, unconstrained multi-part assembly, or arbitrary tactile hardware.

## Negative-result information

If explicit estimation fails to improve matched control despite measured state accuracy, the field should prioritize control representation or action design. If neither approach transfers, the evidence localizes the limiting factor to the chosen sensing/contact regime or test distribution.
