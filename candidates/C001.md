# Candidate C001

## Source uncertainty

**Problem-map node:** U01

## Scientific question

When sensory information is held constant, are next-fixation choices governed mainly by a cost-sensitive belief-state objective, a bounded approximation to information gain, or history-dependent heuristics?

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

The existing task-specific deviations from pure information gain do not identify whether they reflect intrinsic action cost, computational/memory limits, or a non-value-based choice policy.

## Established evidence

Priors, explicit costs, and sequential evidence change gaze behavior; pure information-gain policies miss some behavior. Constrained Bayesian models improve fits in artificial search. These observations do not identify the internal objective or algorithm.

## Relevant explanations

### H1

A cost-sensitive belief-state policy weights expected error/information together with independently measured intrinsic saccade effort and time cost.

### H2

A bounded information-gain policy selects from an approximate or memory-limited belief state; calibrated effort variation has no residual effect after motor variability and latency are accounted for.

### H3

A history/heuristic policy is driven mainly by repeat, proximity, or fixed scan tendencies and predicts weak trial-by-trial sensitivity to the manipulated payoff of information.

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Prior studies jointly vary task penalties, motor demands, information geometry, and response history. Fitted parameters can absorb these variables.

## Potential discriminating observation

In a gaze-contingent three-location hidden-target task, independently calibrate direction-specific saccade effort and landing variability, then factorially vary information value and imposed switch cost while preserving target likelihoods. Compare out-of-sample next-fixation probabilities and sequences. H1 predicts an effort effect beyond variability and history that changes with information value; H2 predicts effects mediated by variability/belief approximation; H3 predicts stable history effects and poor payoff-sensitive generalization.

## Provisional attack

Use within-participant calibration blocks for vector-specific effort proxies and motor error, followed by counterbalanced search blocks with identical visual evidence. Fit preregistered policy families and test held-out conditions designed so the models reverse their preferred first fixation.

## Why now

Gaze-contingent displays and simultaneous eye tracking permit separate measurement of choices, landing variability, latency, and pupil-linked effort; recent work makes intrinsic effort a concrete competing variable.

## Scope

Human, head-restrained, screen-based target search. It addresses algorithmic action selection in this regime and does not claim ecological or circuit generality.

## Negative-result information

Failure of calibrated effort to improve held-out prediction would constrain intrinsic-effort explanations in this task; failure of every specified policy would reject the proposed decomposition and motivate unmodeled state variables.

## Evidence basis

### Problem-map sections

U01; explanatory families; constraint-weighted belief-state control.

### Paper cards

Ahmad et al. 2014; Morvan & Maloney 2012; Yang et al. 2016; Zhou & Yu 2021; Koevoet et al. 2025.

### Original sources requiring verification

None required for the framing; implementation must validate the effort calibration independently.

## Unverified assumptions

Pupil-linked and behavioral calibration can identify a direction-specific action-cost construct separately from arousal, visual asymmetry, and motor uncertainty.

## Construct validity and surviving implementation alternatives

- **Scientific construct identified by the proposed discriminator:** The algorithmic weighting of expected information/error against intrinsic action cost in next-fixation selection.
- **Implementation-level explanations that could produce the same outcome:** Direction-dependent motor noise, anisotropic visual sensitivity, pupil-linked arousal, a response threshold, or unmodeled muscular mechanics.
- **What this experiment would not identify even if successful:** The neural locus or biophysical representation of cost; generality to natural eye-head-body behavior; a uniquely Bayesian neural implementation.
