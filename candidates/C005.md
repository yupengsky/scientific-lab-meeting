## Scientific question

When context use fails under order, calibration, long-context, or adversarial perturbations, do the failures arise from estimator instability, positional/circuit competition, or reliance on prior/format shortcuts?

---

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

The mechanism of conditional ICL failures and whether distinct perturbations reveal a common failure process.

---

## Established evidence

- ICL is sensitive to order, calibration, label space, and prompt format (Lu 2022; Zhao 2021).
- Long-context gains can depend strongly on similarity/retrieval, and restricted models show adversarial context hijacking (Bertsch 2024; Anwar 2024).

---

## Relevant explanations

### H1
Failures reflect instability of the context-computed estimator or task state when examples are noisy, reordered, or contradictory.

### H2
Failures primarily reflect positional or circuit competition that changes which context information reaches the query computation.

### H3
Failures primarily expose shortcut reliance on priors, format, or retrieval rather than a stable task-learning computation.

---

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

Different perturbations may invoke different mechanisms, but matched perturbation signatures can test whether one explanation dominates a regime.

---

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Robustness results depend on perturbation budget, context length, label space, architecture, and metric. Existing findings do not align these variables or measure internal computation during failure.

---

## Potential discriminating observation

Under factorial order, noise, position, retrieval, and semantic-prior controls, compare failure curves with estimator-state probes, positional/path interventions, and mapping-sensitive tests. H1 predicts degradation proportional to estimator inconsistency; H2 predicts position/path-specific effects; H3 predicts persistence under mapping corruption and strong sensitivity to priors or nearest-example availability.

---

## Provisional attack

Use controlled perturbation suites with activation patching and path ablations, matched short/long contexts, and separate mapping, retrieval, and prior probes.

---

## Why now

Existing robustness, long-context, and adversarial-context paradigms can be combined with causal component and intermediate-state measurements.

---

## Scope

Selected transformer LMs on few-shot classification and synthetic sequence tasks across short and long contexts. The conclusions concern tested perturbation regimes, not general safety or all failure modes.

---

## Negative-result information

If no common signature appears, the result would show that “ICL failure” is mechanistically heterogeneous and constrain broad robustness claims. If one signature transfers across perturbations, it would identify a more general failure mechanism.

# Lab meeting

## Hamming
VERDICT: QUESTIONABLE

CORE QUESTION:

If solved completely, would a matched causal analysis of ICL failures reveal a transferable failure mechanism that changes how scientists explain, predict, or design context use across perturbation regimes?

WHY IT MATTERS:

The underlying uncertainty is scientifically important. Current evidence shows that order, calibration, retrieval dependence, and adversarial context manipulation can alter behavior, but does not identify a common internal cause. Resolving whether failures reflect estimator instability, positional/circuit competition, or shortcut reliance could constrain claims about what ICL computes and make robustness interventions more principled.

WHAT CHANGES IF SOLVED:

- A supported common mechanism would improve causal understanding of context use and identify variables that predict failure beyond a single benchmark or attack.
- A supported heterogeneous account would delimit the meaning of “ICL failure,” prevent inappropriate generalization from one perturbation, and guide future interventions and robustness tests.
- Mechanistic evidence could distinguish genuine task/mapping computation from failures caused primarily by priors, format, or retrieval in the tested regimes.

WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE:

- It would not by itself establish a universal theory of ICL, resolve the causal core of successful ICL, or show transfer to all large language models, tasks, or open-ended generation.
- A collection of perturbation-specific signatures would mainly organize known behavioral sensitivities unless it yields predictive and intervention-validated consequences.
- The contribution is not important merely because these perturbations have not previously been combined; the value depends on discriminating explanations at a causal level.

STRONGEST SO-WHAT OBJECTION:

Even a successful study may conclude only that different attacks produce different internal disruptions in selected models and synthetic/classification tasks. That would be a difficult technical characterization with limited scientific leverage: existing evidence already establishes conditional brittleness, while the proposed hypotheses span partially different explanatory levels and may all be true in different regimes. Without a clear cross-regime prediction or intervention that changes failure behavior, the result may not materially alter theory or future experimental practice.

FATAL IMPORTANCE FLAW:
none

## Medawar
VERDICT: WEAK ATTACK

REASONABLE ATTACK:

Run a small, pre-registered factorial study on one synthetic mapping task and one few-shot classification task, varying order, example noise, query/example position, retrieval similarity, and label mapping while holding the underlying task fixed. Measure behavior after interventions designed to remove priors and retrieval, then test whether a small set of prespecified intermediate-state or component interventions predicts and selectively reverses each failure signature. The current proposal names these ingredients, but it does not yet specify the state variables, paths, or intervention outcomes that would decisively distinguish H1, H2, and H3.

WHY NOW:

The field now has experimentally manipulable failure factors and candidate causal measurement tools. Lu 2022 establishes order sensitivity under fixed examples, Zhao 2021 provides measurable calibration interventions, Bertsch 2024 provides retrieval-sensitive long-context comparisons, and Anwar 2024 demonstrates architecture- and attack-dependent prompt hijacking. The problem map also records task-vector, component, and causal-intervention work. These developments make a bounded mechanistic pilot possible. They do not yet make a broad common-mechanism claim tractable.

MINIMUM TRACTABLE QUESTION:

In one controlled task family, after calibration and retrieval are independently controlled, does one perturbation class produce a reproducible internal signature that predicts failure and is selectively rescued by a matched intervention? A result should be stated for the tested models and perturbations, without claiming one mechanism across all ICL failures.

MINIMUM FIRST STEP:

Build a 2×2 pilot crossing order perturbation with mapping corruption on a synthetic arbitrary-label task, using fixed models, prompts, and compute. Pre-register behavioral outcomes, a mapping-sensitive probe, and one candidate state/component intervention. Include clean, calibration-controlled, and retrieval-matched conditions. The decisive criterion is selective change in the predicted signature and output, with matched generic-damage and shuffled-control interventions.

MAIN TECHNICAL BOTTLENECK:

Separating mechanism-specific causal effects from generic attention disruption and correlated prompt factors. Activation patching, path ablation, calibration, retrieval, position, and mapping interventions can all alter the computation simultaneously; a probe that decodes an estimator state or task vector does not establish that the state is the operative cause. The full factorial across short/long contexts, multiple perturbations, tasks, and models also creates a large multiplicity and compute burden before any hypothesis is decisively tested.

CHEAPER ATTACK:

Start with synthetic arbitrary-label regression or classification, two perturbation axes, and one model family. Use behavioral counterfactuals plus a single independently validated mapping-state intervention. Defer long-context retrieval and adversarial attacks until the state signature survives this pilot. This preserves the core uncertainty about estimator instability versus shortcut reliance at much lower cost.

FATAL TRACTABILITY FLAW:

The broad version is currently underdetermined: H1, H2, and H3 can coexist, and the proposed observations do not specify mutually exclusive predictions or a validated causal intervention for each. Without a narrowed task, fixed perturbation budget, prespecified internal variables, and selective rescue criterion, the project can generate many interpretable failure curves without reducing the mechanism uncertainty. This is a repairable flaw, so it does not justify CURRENTLY INSOLUBLE.

CONFIDENCE:

high

NEEDS_VERIFICATION:

- Source: `literature/cards/bertsch_2024.md`; detail: full-text dataset/model coverage, perturbation controls, and quantitative basis for “most gains” being similarity-based; impact: determines whether retrieval is a justified branch of the proposed factorial mechanism test or only a setting-specific confound.
- Source: `literature/cards/anwar_2024.md`; detail: exact attack budgets, optimization procedures, model scales, and transfer conditions; impact: determines whether adversarial hijacking supplies a comparable perturbation regime or mainly motivates a separate robustness study.
- Source: `literature/cards/lu_2022.md`; detail: whether order effects were jointly controlled for recency, label-position statistics, verbalizer priors, and prompt semantics; impact: determines how much order sensitivity remains mechanistically unresolved.
- Source: `literature/cards/zhao_2021.md`; detail: whether contextual calibration removes the relevant failure modes across the proposed task families; impact: if calibration explains most effects, the shortcut branch and the pilot design must be revised.

## Platt
PHENOMENON:

Few-shot predictions can change substantially when demonstrations are reordered, calibrated, lengthened, made noisy or contradictory, retrieved by similarity, or adversarially perturbed. The observation requiring explanation is conditional failure of context use. The cards establish the behavioral effect; they do not establish that these perturbations share an internal cause.

COMPETING HYPOTHESES:

- H1 — estimator/state instability: perturbations make the context-derived estimate or task state internally inconsistent, producing proportionate degradation as evidence quality declines.
- H2 — positional/path competition: perturbations alter which positions, heads, or computation paths deliver information to the query. Effects should depend on location and architecture, with selective path interventions changing failure curves.
- H3 — shortcut/ prior reliance: apparent context use is partly driven by label priors, format, memorized task recognition, or nearest-example retrieval. Mapping corruption, prior controls, or removal of similar examples should disproportionately affect performance.
- H4 — heterogeneous/composite failure: the mechanisms coexist and different perturbations recruit different routes. This is a serious alternative because the problem map explicitly treats retrieval, task inference, mapping, verbalization, and circuit effects as compatible or conditional accounts.

PREDICTION TABLE:

| Outcome / measurement | H1 | H2 | H3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graded noise/contradiction | Smooth degradation tracking estimator inconsistency | Depends on where conflicting examples are placed and on path access | May remain stable if priors/format dominate, or fail when shortcuts are disrupted |
| Permutation and position controls | Similar effect after controlling evidence content | Strong location-, recency-, and path-specific effect | Effect tracks label position, format, or verbalizer priors |
| Arbitrary mapping corruption | Internal estimator/task-state change and output degradation | Effect only when the relevant mapping path is disrupted | Large degradation if prior/format shortcut is removed; small degradation if mapping was never used |
| Retrieval removal or similarity matching | Degradation proportional to lost estimator evidence | Position/path mediation dominates | Large selective effect if nearest-example retrieval is the shortcut |
| Causal probe/ablation result | Selective intervention on estimator state changes predictions | Selective path intervention changes predictions | Prior/format intervention changes predictions |

The table does not yet yield cleanly divergent predictions. H1 can implement position-sensitive computation; H2 can cause an apparently unstable estimator; H3 can coexist with genuine mapping; and H4 can explain mixed signatures. Probe decodability or activation patching alone therefore cannot identify the mechanism.

PROPOSED CRITICAL EXPERIMENT:

The proposed factorial perturbation suite, activation patching, and path ablations is directionally relevant, but it is under-specified as a critical experiment. It must predefine which quantitative pattern distinguishes the hypotheses, use matched synthetic tasks with independently randomized mapping, priors, similarity, order, and position, and include clean negative controls for intervention damage. State probes must be trained and tested on held-out counterfactual mappings; ablations must be compared with magnitude- and performance-matched control ablations.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME ELIMINATES:

- A selective mapping effect after equalizing priors, format, similarity, and position weakens H3 as a complete explanation, while leaving H1/H2/H4 viable.
- A selective position/path effect with preserved task evidence weakens a purely content-based H1, while leaving H2 and composite accounts viable.
- A transferable estimator-state intervention that changes predictions without generic loss damage weakens H2-only and H3-only accounts, provided the state is independently validated.
- Different perturbations producing non-overlapping signatures eliminate the claim of one common failure mechanism, but support H4 rather than selecting among the underlying mechanisms.
- A null effect does not eliminate H1–H4 unless manipulation validity and intervention sensitivity are demonstrated.

SURVIVING ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS:

Redundant distributed computation, task-recognition versus mapping being sequential, verbalization errors, prompt-template semantics, tokenization, finite attack budgets, and generic distribution shift can all survive the proposed tests. A probe can decode a consequence rather than a causal state; an ablation can create nonspecific damage; and matched behavioral curves do not imply matched mechanisms.

NEGATIVE-RESULT VALUE:

A robust absence of a common signature would constrain the broad claim that “ICL failure” has one mechanism and support mechanistic heterogeneity within the tested regimes. It would not show that any individual hypothesis is false. Negative evidence is informative only after demonstrating that each perturbation changed its intended variable and that the probes/interventions had adequate sensitivity.

BETTER DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT:

Use a small set of synthetic classification/regression tasks with orthogonal counterfactual manipulations: independently randomize label mapping, class priors, example similarity, order, and absolute position. For each condition, measure output, a held-out task-state decoder, and a causal interchange intervention that swaps the inferred mapping while preserving format and priors. Add position/path interventions matched to the same behavioral damage and a no-op intervention control. The decisive test is whether mapping, position, and prior interventions produce separable, cross-condition causal effects rather than merely separable correlations. Replicate across short/long contexts and at least two architectures before claiming transfer.

DISCRIMINATION STRENGTH:
- PARTIAL

VERDICT:
- REDESIGN

FATAL INFERENCE FLAW:

The candidate treats failure signatures and probe/ablation effects as if they uniquely identify H1, H2, or H3, although the hypotheses are mechanistically compatible and the proposed measurements are non-unique. A factorial perturbation result can be explained post hoc by H4 or by a downstream representation shared by several mechanisms.

HIGH

- Source: `literature/cards/bertsch_2024.md`; detail: full-text controls and quantitative evidence behind the “most gains” retrieval interpretation; impact: if retrieval tests are already mechanistically discriminating, the candidate’s novelty and H3 branch change.
- Source: `literature/cards/anwar_2024.md`; detail: attack budgets, adaptive optimization, and scale/architecture transfer protocols; impact: setting-specific attacks would limit the generality of the proposed adversarial branch.

## Alon
SCIENTIFIC INTEREST:
high

FEASIBILITY:
medium

EXPECTED KNOWLEDGE GAIN:
Potentially high if the study establishes a transferable, causally validated failure signature. The current design is more likely to produce a regime map of conditional brittleness than a general mechanism, because U6 evidence is explicitly scope-dependent and H1–H3 can coexist. A bounded result could still clarify which perturbations alter mapping, retrieval, or prior use in the tested models.

VALUE IF MAIN HYPOTHESIS IS WRONG:
Medium. A null common signature would be useful evidence against a single-mechanism account and would support mechanistic heterogeneity. Its value is limited if probes and interventions are insensitive or nonselective, since a null could then mean measurement failure rather than absence of a common process.

COST:
High for the proposed scope. The factorial perturbation suite, short/long contexts, multiple tasks and models, activation patching, path ablations, and controls create substantial compute, analysis, and multiplicity costs.

TECHNICAL RISK:
High. Activation probes can decode consequences rather than causes, and path interventions can cause generic computational damage. Separating position, retrieval, mapping, and prior manipulations requires controls that are difficult to make orthogonal.

CONCEPTUAL RISK:
High. The hypotheses are partially competing but also mechanistically compatible and span different explanatory levels. Similar failure curves or intervention effects may therefore support several explanations, while heterogeneous results do not identify which mechanism caused each failure.

BETTER SMALLER VERSION:
Run a preregistered 2×2 pilot on one synthetic arbitrary-label task and one model family: order/no-order crossed with mapping-preserved/mapping-corrupted examples, with priors, format, and example similarity held fixed. Use one held-out mapping-state decoder and one independently validated interchange or rescue intervention, plus magnitude-matched generic-damage controls. Advance to retrieval, long-context, adversarial, and second-architecture conditions only if the pilot shows a selective causal signature.

EXPECTED RESEARCH VALUE:
medium

VERDICT:
- PILOT FIRST

FATAL VALUE FLAW:
The full project can be technically impressive while failing to resolve its central question: a broad set of perturbation-specific signatures would still be compatible with H1, H2, H3, or a composite account. Without a selective intervention whose result changes the explanation or predicts/rescues failure across conditions, most of the added factorial scope buys characterization rather than decisive knowledge.
