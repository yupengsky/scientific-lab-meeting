## Scientific question

Which training-distribution and optimization variables cause a model to use in-context computation rather than in-weights solutions, and do observed transitions reflect circuit competition or a shared representation phase change?

---

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

The causal mechanism behind emergence, persistence, and disappearance of ICL during training.

---

## Established evidence

- Data-distribution manipulations change the utility of ICL and in-weights learning (Chan 2022).
- Training can show transient tradeoffs and circuit formation dynamics (Singh 2023; Olsson 2022; Singh 2024).

---

## Relevant explanations

### H1
ICL and in-weights learning compete for explanatory or representational resources, so changing one solution’s availability shifts mechanism use.

### H2
The transition is primarily a shared optimization/representation phase change, so mechanism timing tracks training dynamics even when solution competition is controlled.

---

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

They predict different responses to interventions that hold data utility fixed while altering circuit availability and optimization conditions.

---

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Current studies confound data utility, training stage, architecture, regularization, and measured ICL. Stable macro performance can conceal a changing mechanism.

---

## Potential discriminating observation

In matched training runs, manipulate data utility and selectively disable or preserve candidate ICL/in-weights paths. H1 predicts reciprocal changes when one solution is made unavailable; H2 predicts transition timing tied mainly to optimization and representation variables, with weaker reciprocal dependence.

---

## Provisional attack

Train controlled transformer families with factorial distribution mixtures, curriculum timing, regularization, and checkpoint interventions; measure behavior, in-weights scores, and causal circuit/state metrics over training.

---

## Why now

Checkpoint-scale analysis and circuit-formation interventions allow mechanism trajectories to be measured rather than inferred from endpoint behavior alone.

---

## Scope

Controlled synthetic sequence and classification training in small-to-medium transformers, with carefully defined ICL and in-weights metrics. It does not establish training laws for all frontier LMs.

---

## Negative-result information

If circuit availability does not shift the balance, competition is weakened and the field gains constraints on interpreting transient tradeoffs. If transitions persist under fixed utility and path availability, a shared optimization/representation explanation remains viable.

## Platt

PHENOMENON:

In the cited synthetic transformer studies, holdout-class ICL can emerge and later decline while in-weights learning increases and loss continues to improve. Distributional manipulations and regularization change these trajectories. The observation requiring explanation is a changing balance between context-dependent and weight-dependent solutions under continued training.

COMPETING HYPOTHESES:

### H1 — Resource/circuit competition

ICL and in-weights learning use partially competing representational or circuit resources. If one route is selectively made unavailable while task utility is held fixed, the other route should compensate.

### H2 — Shared optimization or representation transition

A common training-stage variable changes the accessibility or attractiveness of both routes. The apparent tradeoff follows optimization and representation dynamics, without requiring reciprocal competition.

### H3 — Measurement/distribution artifact

The evaluators or synthetic data regime create the apparent tradeoff through memorization, label priors, evaluator distribution, or task-specific statistics. The underlying mechanisms need not change reciprocally.

PREDICTION TABLE:

| Outcome / measurement | H1 | H2 | H3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selective ICL-path suppression at matched utility | ICL falls and IWL rises reproducibly | ICL falls; IWL need not rise and timing follows training stage | Scores change idiosyncratically or only for the particular evaluator |
| Selective IWL-path suppression | IWL falls and ICL rises | IWL falls; ICL follows shared phase variables | Apparent rescue varies with probe/distribution |
| Same intervention across checkpoints and seeds | Reciprocal effects are stable and route-specific | Effects depend mainly on checkpoint/optimization state | Effects are unstable, probe-specific, or non-replicable |
| Evaluator swap plus causal mechanism probes | Reciprocity persists | Timing/representation changes persist without reciprocity | The effect disappears or changes when the evaluator changes |

The proposed experiment has divergent predictions only after selective path manipulation and evaluator validity are established. A broad factorial sweep over distributions, curricula, regularization, checkpoints, and circuit interventions allows post hoc attribution and is therefore weakly discriminating.

PROPOSED CRITICAL EXPERIMENT:

First validate one reversible ICL-path intervention and one IWL-path intervention at fixed checkpoints. Require route-specific effects on orthogonal probes, with sham controls and prespecified tolerances for loss, gradient norms, capacity, and unrelated behavior. Then run a small two-by-two route-availability experiment at early and late checkpoints under one matched data distribution. Test the preregistered interaction: suppressing one route must selectively increase the other, across seeds and evaluator swaps, after matching task utility.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME ELIMINATES:

- Stable reciprocal compensation under selective, optimization-matched interventions weakens H2 and H3 and supports H1 within this model/task regime.
- Suppression of one route without reciprocal increase, with transition timing tracking checkpoint or regularization, weakens H1 and supports H2.
- Effects that disappear under evaluator swaps or matched probes weaken the mechanism claim and support H3.
- A null result after a validated selective intervention weakens competition for the targeted routes. It does not eliminate redundant-route competition, competition expressed through data utility, or H2.

SURVIVING ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS:

Redundant circuits can mask a true competition effect. A clamp or ablation can alter gradients, effective capacity, or optimization trajectory. ICL and IWL may be compatible strategies whose measured tradeoff reflects changing utility rather than resource conflict. The two hypotheses may also operate at different levels: a circuit response can be downstream of a shared phase transition. Synthetic Omniglot-like tasks may expose mechanisms that do not transfer to language models. The cited cards explicitly leave evaluator validity, intervention locality, and transfer unresolved.

NEGATIVE-RESULT VALUE:

A negative reciprocal effect is informative only if the intervention demonstrably removes the intended route and leaves optimization sufficiently unchanged. Under those conditions it constrains strong claims of mutual circuit substitution in the tested regime. A null result from a nonlocal or unvalidated intervention eliminates little.

BETTER DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT:

Use the intervention-validation matrix as a go/no-go pilot: matched checkpoints, multiple seeds, intact/sham/suppressed conditions, orthogonal ICL/IWL probes, evaluator swaps, and direct measurements of loss and gradients. Proceed only if each intervention has route-selective causal effects. This is cheaper and more decisive than the proposed full factorial study.

DISCRIMINATION STRENGTH:
- PARTIAL

VERDICT:
- REDESIGN

FATAL INFERENCE FLAW:

Reciprocal changes in proxy scores after broad circuit or training interventions do not identify competition. The same pattern can result from altered optimization, evaluator shift, redundancy, or a shared representation transition. The current design does not cleanly separate H1 from H2.

CONFIDENCE:

High for the underdetermination critique; medium for the redesigned experiment because intervention selectivity is unverified.

NEEDS_VERIFICATION:
