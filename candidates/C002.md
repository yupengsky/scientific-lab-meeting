# Candidate C002

## Source uncertainty

**Problem-map node:** U02

## Scientific question

Does the external-versus-saccade uncertainty dissociation arise from uncertainty source, categorical report learning, or a source-specific Bayesian likelihood?

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

The behavioral dissociation in transsaccadic displacement judgments has several compatible computational explanations and no identified mechanism.

## Established evidence

External blur reduced categorical prior use in the cited paradigm, whereas saccade-related uncertainty and continuous reports showed Bayesian-consistent patterns. Simulations favoring discriminative or hybrid accounts remain non-unique.

## Relevant explanations

### H1

Bayesian source-specific likelihoods: matched uncertainty sources yield different prior weighting because their sensory likelihoods or causal structure differ.

### H2

Discriminative categorical learning: categorical report mappings generate anti-Bayesian prior use under external blur regardless of source when category learning is emphasized.

### H3

Report-rule account: the dissociation follows categorical versus continuous decision mapping, without a distinct uncertainty-source inference route.

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

## Why current evidence is insufficient

The existing study combines source manipulations with response format and trained priors. Alternative likelihood parameterizations and report rules were not exhaustively discriminated.

## Potential discriminating observation

Cross external blur and calibrated displacement/saccade-related uncertainty with categorical and continuous reports, using matched sensory discriminability and counterbalanced prior-training regimes. Test whether prior weighting tracks source after report format and category training are held fixed. H1 predicts residual source dependence; H2 predicts a category-learning interaction across sources; H3 predicts report-format dependence with minimal residual source effect.

## Provisional attack

Obtain psychometric calibration for each uncertainty manipulation, use a within-subject factorial design, and compare preregistered hierarchical models by out-of-sample predictions for both reports and confidence.

## Why now

The source/report dissociation is explicit in current evidence and modern gaze-contingent display control can calibrate perceptual discriminability across conditions.

## Scope

Human transsaccadic displacement judgments. It studies perceptual inference and report, not next-fixation policy, general active search, or neural localization.

## Negative-result information

Absence of source dependence after calibration would weaken a source-specific inference claim; absence of report-format dependence would weaken a report-rule explanation. Failure to distinguish models would show that the proposed observables remain insufficient.

## Construct validity and surviving implementation alternatives

- **Scientific construct identified by the proposed discriminator:** The algorithmic source and format dependence of prior weighting in transsaccadic perception.
- **Implementation-level explanations that could produce the same outcome:** Unequal sensory discriminability, saccade timing/suppression differences, criterion shifts, unequal prior learning, attention, or motor-report mapping.
- **What this experiment would not identify even if successful:** A neural source classifier, a distinct circuit, or generalization from displacement judgments to active visual search.
