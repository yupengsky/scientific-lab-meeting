## Scientific question

Under matched prompts and arbitrary-label mappings, when does changing the demonstrated mapping alter a context-derived task state and output beyond effects explained by label priors, task recognition, or similar-example retrieval?

---

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

Which component of measured ICL—mapping acquisition, task recognition, retrieval, or pretrained/format priors—drives the gain in controlled task settings.

---

## Established evidence

- Prompt format, verbalizers, calibration, and input distributions materially affect ICL measurements (Min 2022; Zhao 2021; Webson & Pavlick 2022).
- Other controlled evidence supports task inference and arbitrary-label mapping, while long-context gains can be similarity-dependent (Tao 2024; Bertsch 2024).

---

## Relevant explanations

### H1
The demonstrated input-output mapping is acquired and causally changes an internal task state that controls the query answer.

### H2
The measured gain is primarily task recognition, label/format priors, or pretrained knowledge; changing the mapping has little independent causal effect.

### H3
The gain is primarily retrieval of similar demonstrations, with mapping effects limited to cases where retrieval supplies the relevant relation.

---

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

These factors can coexist, but matched interventions can estimate their independent contributions.

---

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Label semantics, prompt format, pretrained knowledge, similarity, and mapping are correlated in common benchmarks. Existing controls usually remove one confounder or test one task family rather than jointly separating all factors.

---

## Potential discriminating observation

Across counterbalanced arbitrary-label remappings, semantic-neutral prompts, similarity-matched demonstrations, and corrupted mappings, compare output changes, task-state probes, and answer-versus-verbalization interventions. H1 predicts mapping-specific state and answer changes even when labels and similarity are controlled; H2 predicts persistence under mapping corruption but sensitivity to format/priors; H3 predicts dependence on nearest-example availability.

---

## Provisional attack

Use factorial prompt interventions with random labels, semantic-neutral verbalizers, retrieval-matched examples, mapping swaps, and interchange/activation-patching measurements for task and verbalization states.

---

## Why now

Existing arbitrary-label, calibration, long-context retrieval, and inference/verbalization methods provide complementary controls that can be combined into a causal decomposition.

---

## Scope

Few-shot classification and synthetic input-output mapping in selected open LMs, with retrieval-controlled contexts and bounded context lengths. It does not generalize to every open-ended generation task.

---

## Negative-result information

Failure to find an independent mapping effect would show that the benchmark gain is dominated by recognition, retrieval, or priors in the tested regime and would prevent calling that gain task learning. A null effect under one prompt family would not rule out mapping acquisition elsewhere.

- Task-state probes distinguish mapping from correlated recognition states.

# Lab meeting

## Hamming
VERDICT: IMPORTANT

CORE QUESTION:

Does changing the demonstrated input-output mapping causally alter task computation and output once label priors, prompt format, task recognition, and similarity-based retrieval are controlled?

WHY IT MATTERS:

This addresses what existing ICL measurements actually measure. Current evidence shows that format, verbalizers, priors, task recognition, and retrieval can produce substantial performance changes, while other controlled results support separable inference and verbalization. The unresolved separation is central to interpreting claims of “task learning” and to deciding which mechanistic theories and experiments deserve emphasis.

WHAT CHANGES IF SOLVED:

If mapping acquisition has an independent causal effect, the field gains evidence for a distinct context-learning component and a sharper target for representation and circuit studies. If it does not in the tested regime, many benchmark gains should be reinterpreted as recognition, retrieval, or prior/format effects. Either result would materially improve explanation, theory selection, causal interpretation of ICL, and the design of future experiments.

WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE:

The result would not establish a universal mechanism for all ICL, open-ended generation, or every model and task. It would not by itself identify the complete internal circuit or settle broader disputes about Bayesian versus optimization accounts. Its value is diagnostic and foundational rather than an immediate capability improvement.

STRONGEST SO-WHAT OBJECTION:

This may be a careful decomposition of benchmark performance whose conclusions remain confined to selected synthetic and classification settings. A null or regime-specific mapping effect could leave practical model behavior unchanged and add another taxonomy to an already fragmented literature.

FATAL IMPORTANCE FLAW:
[none]

CONFIDENCE:
medium

NEEDS_VERIFICATION:
- Wei et al., INDEX-only arbitrary-label findings: the exact tasks, controls, and strength of evidence for mapping acquisition are not available in a local paper card. If those findings already jointly isolate the mapping contribution, the candidate’s central uncertainty and importance would be substantially reduced.
- Tao 2024, Sections 2.2–3.3: the card reports causal separation of inference and verbalization but notes model-, task-, and layer-specific limits. If the full evidence already directly answers the proposed matched-remapping question across the relevant regime, the claimed gap would narrow.
- Bertsch 2024: the card is marked PARTIAL and its “most gains” retrieval interpretation requires full-text verification. If its controls quantitatively establish the relevant separation, the candidate’s retrieval-related motivation would weaken.

## Medawar
VERDICT: WEAK ATTACK

REASONABLE ATTACK:
Use a small, counterbalanced synthetic classification experiment in which the same inputs and demonstrations are paired with arbitrary label permutations and corrupted mappings, while matching example similarity and measuring both behavior and a preregistered causal intervention on a candidate intermediate state. The attack must compare mapping-sensitive versus mapping-insensitive conditions within the same model, task, and prompt family. This can test whether output changes track the demonstrated relation rather than label priors or nearest-example retrieval.

WHY NOW:
The cited work supplies complementary ingredients: prompt and verbalizer effects (Min 2022; Zhao 2021; Webson & Pavlick 2022), label-remapping/interchange evidence for separable inference and verbalization (Tao 2024), and evidence that similarity can dominate long-context gains (Bertsch 2024). These results make a targeted causal decomposition possible. They do not yet establish that all proposed factors can be independently manipulated in one design.

MINIMUM TRACTABLE QUESTION:
In one synthetic task family and a small, specified set of open models, does changing an arbitrary demonstrated input-output mapping change query predictions after controlling for label-token priors, prompt format, and nearest-example similarity?

MINIMUM FIRST STEP:
Run a preregistered 2x2 experiment: correct versus permuted demonstration mappings, crossed with query examples that are either retrieval-matched or retrieval-mismatched, using fixed neutral labels and balanced label frequencies. The primary outcome should be the within-input change in the query answer, with a no-demonstration and corrupted-mapping baseline. Establish this behavioral separation before adding probes or activation patching.

MAIN TECHNICAL BOTTLENECK:
The factors are not cleanly separable. Random labels can change token priors and prompt coherence; retrieval matching can itself reveal the task relation; and task-state probes can decode a correlated recognition or verbalization state without identifying mapping acquisition. The proposed factorial design therefore risks producing interpretable-looking effects without a uniquely identified causal component. Activation patching adds off-target and layer-selection problems, especially when the candidate treats a distributed state as a single task state.

CHEAPER ATTACK:
Drop the full factorial and mechanistic measurements initially. Use balanced synthetic relations with novel input symbols, arbitrary labels, matched nearest-neighbor structure, and mapping swaps across many held-out queries. Compare exact mapping sensitivity against label-prior and retrieval baselines. A robust mapping effect would justify a second-stage causal representation study; a null or retrieval-explained effect would already answer the minimum scientific question for the tested regime.

FATAL TRACTABILITY FLAW:
None for the narrowed behavioral question. In its current form, the broad claim that mapping, recognition, retrieval, priors, and an internal task state can all be independently decomposed is not identified by the stated controls. The project needs a narrower primary estimand and an explicit decision rule for what observation distinguishes H1 from H2/H3.

CONFIDENCE:
High for the tractability assessment; moderate for the evidence-specific assessment because the cited Tao (2024) and Bertsch (2024) cards are preprints/partial or model- and task-bounded evidence.

NEEDS_VERIFICATION:
- Tao 2024, Sections 2.2–3.3: exact interchange intervention design, effect sizes, and robustness. This could change the verdict if the interventions already provide a validated mapping-specific causal measure that directly resolves the proposed identification problem.
- Bertsch 2024, long-context scaling experiments: exact retrieval controls and quantitative support for the “most gains” interpretation. This could change how strongly retrieval must be controlled, though it is unlikely to remove the need for a minimal within-task test.
- Candidate evidence basis, “controlled arbitrary-label findings in the INDEX record for Wei et al.”: exact task, labels, and controls are not present in the cited local cards. This could change the baseline plausibility of H1 and the design of the first experiment.

## Platt
PHENOMENON:

In controlled few-shot classification, changing or randomizing demonstration labels can have a small average effect, while format, verbalizers, priors, and similarity can materially affect accuracy. Some arbitrary-label experiments also report separable inference and verbalization effects. The unresolved phenomenon is whether a demonstrated mapping makes an independent causal contribution to the query computation in a regime where these factors are matched.

COMPETING HYPOTHESES:

- H1: The mapping is acquired and changes a context-derived task state that causally affects the answer.
- H2: The apparent gain is explained by task recognition, label/format priors, pretrained knowledge, or verbalization. Mapping information has little independent effect.
- H3: The gain is mainly retrieval of similar demonstrations. Mapping matters only when retrieved examples contain the relevant relation.

PREDICTION TABLE:

| Outcome / measurement | H1 | H2 | H3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counterbalanced mapping swap with semantic-neutral labels and matched examples | Query answer changes in mapping-consistent directions, including for non-nearest examples | Little answer change after calibration; residual effects track format/priors | Change concentrated on queries with relevant similar demonstrations |
| Corrupted mapping while preserving retrieval similarity | Performance and mapping-sensitive state degrade | Performance remains near intact or follows priors/recognition | Performance degrades only when corruption affects useful retrieved relations |
| Retrieval-matched but relation-informative versus relation-uninformative contexts | Mapping-sensitive state/answer differs | Little difference after recognition and prior controls | Difference tracks availability of similar informative examples |
| Intervention on a purported task state | Selective change in answer under fixed verbalization | No selective effect, or effect reflects a general recognition/representation change | Effect depends on retrieved-example content |
| Answer-versus-verbalization intervention | Mapping manipulation changes answer representation before label coding | Effects appear mainly at label/output coding | Effects follow the retrieved example path and may appear at either stage |

PROPOSED CRITICAL EXPERIMENT:

Run a factorial, counterbalanced intervention over arbitrary-label mappings, mapping corruption, semantic-neutral verbalizers, calibrated output priors, and retrieval-matched demonstrations; measure behavioral answers, label outputs, and activation interchange/patching at task and verbalization locations.

This is directionally relevant, but it is not yet a single critical experiment. The design must pre-register a contrast in which retrieval evidence is held fixed while mapping informativeness changes, and must define an answer-level readout independent of the model’s labels. Without that contrast, all three hypotheses can accommodate most outcomes.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME ELIMINATES:

- A robust mapping-specific answer change under fixed retrieval, priors, and verbalization weakens H2 and the retrieval-only version of H3; it does not establish a unique internal mechanism for H1.
- No answer change after a validated mapping corruption weakens H1 for the tested tasks and models, but does not eliminate H1 in other regimes.
- Effects limited to similar, relation-informative examples weaken a global mapping account and support H3, while leaving mixed mapping-plus-retrieval accounts alive.
- A task-state intervention that changes answers while preserving label coding weakens pure H2 and pure H3, conditional on intervention specificity.

SURVIVING ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS:

Random labels can introduce tokenization and output-prior changes rather than remove semantic priors. Retrieval matching can preserve hidden relational or lexical cues that encode the mapping. Task recognition and mapping acquisition can be sequential or distributed, so a probe may not distinguish them. Activation patching can transfer correlated states or cause off-target effects. Behavioral nulls can reflect insufficient statistical power, weak corruption, or a task in which mapping is unnecessary. H1 and H3 may coexist, making additive factorial effects underidentified.

NEGATIVE-RESULT VALUE:

A well-powered null mapping effect under independently validated retrieval, prior, and format controls would eliminate the claim that mapping is necessary for the tested benchmark regime. It would not show that models never acquire mappings, nor would it identify recognition or retrieval as the sole surviving cause unless those alternatives were separately manipulated and measured.

BETTER DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT:

Use synthetic tasks with independently controlled factors: query inputs have no useful nearest-neighbor example; demonstrations are either mapping-informative or mapping-uninformative while matched for tokens, format, length, and surface similarity; arbitrary labels are balanced and decoded into an external semantic answer before verbalization. Add a held-out mapping swap and a pre-registered answer-level causal intervention. The decisive contrast is the mapping-information effect at fixed retrieval evidence, followed by the retrieval-information effect at fixed mapping.

DISCRIMINATION STRENGTH:
- PARTIAL

VERDICT:
- REDESIGN

FATAL INFERENCE FLAW:

The proposal treats task-state probes and answer-versus-verbalization interventions as if they uniquely identify mapping acquisition. The cited evidence supports functional separation in selected tasks, but decodability or interchange effects do not distinguish mapping from a correlated recognition or retrieval state. The proposed factorial result therefore lacks a guaranteed divergent prediction across H1–H3.

CONFIDENCE:

Moderate-high. The core underdetermination follows from the candidate’s own stated coexistence of factors and from the limitations in the cited cards.

NEEDS_VERIFICATION:

- `literature/cards/tao_2024.md`, Sections 2.2–3.3, exact interchange effect sizes and robustness: the card states that interventions separate inference and verbalization but flags model/layer-specific limitations; if the effects are weak or non-replicable, the candidate’s strongest evidence for a mapping-sensitive intermediate state is reduced.
- `literature/cards/bertsch_2024.md`, long-context scaling experiments and discussion, full-text verification: the card is marked PARTIAL and does not establish the exact retrieval-matching controls; if those controls already isolate mapping-independent gains, the proposed redesign could be simplified.
- Candidate Evidence basis, INDEX-only Wei et al. arbitrary-label record, exact task and controls: this is explicitly unverified; if the finding used stronger mapping-specific controls than represented here, the assessment of the remaining uncertainty could change.

## Alon
SCIENTIFIC INTEREST:
high

FEASIBILITY:
medium

EXPECTED KNOWLEDGE GAIN:
High if the primary estimand is narrowed to the independent behavioral effect of a demonstrated mapping under fixed retrieval evidence, balanced labels, and calibrated output priors. A decisive result would clarify what standard ICL measurements mean and constrain claims about task learning. The proposed probes and activation patching add mechanistic ambition without proportionate identification power.

VALUE IF MAIN HYPOTHESIS IS WRONG:
Medium-high. A well-powered null, or an effect fully explained by retrieval/priors, would be useful for delimiting task-learning claims in the tested regime. It would not settle whether mapping acquisition occurs in other tasks or models. A technically inconclusive null would have little value.

COST:
Medium-high in the proposed form; medium for the behavioral pilot. The factorial design, multiple models, retrieval matching, causal probes, and activation interventions create substantial implementation and analysis burden.

TECHNICAL RISK:
Medium-high. Random labels may alter tokenization and priors; retrieval matching may leak relation information; activation patching may be off-target; and power requirements rise when effects are small and heterogeneous.

CONCEPTUAL RISK:
High. H1, H2, and H3 can coexist, and a task-state probe or interchange effect does not uniquely identify mapping acquisition. Without a pre-registered answer-level contrast, an impressive result may support only functional separability, not the claimed causal decomposition.

BETTER SMALLER VERSION:
Run one preregistered behavioral pilot on synthetic relations and novel symbols. Cross correct versus permuted/corrupted mappings with retrieval-matched versus retrieval-mismatched contexts, balance label tokens, and measure an externally decoded answer before verbalization. Use within-input mapping sensitivity as the primary estimand, with no-demo, prior-calibration, and retrieval baselines. Stop or redesign if mapping effects are absent, unstable across models, or explained by retrieval. Add probes only after this contrast is decisive.

EXPECTED RESEARCH VALUE:
medium

- PILOT FIRST

FATAL VALUE FLAW:
The full project risks spending most of its effort producing a richer decomposition whose observations remain compatible with multiple explanations. Its value is high only if the mapping-specific contrast is identified behaviorally first; activation-level sophistication cannot repair an underdefined estimand.

CONFIDENCE:
high for the pilot-first recommendation; medium for the precise expected effect size and literature gap.
