# Candidate C002

## Source uncertainty

**Problem-map node:** U01

## Scientific question

Across controlled clearance, pose-error, friction, and stiffness regimes, when does compliance alone prevent jamming and when does feedback provide a measurable reliability or safety advantage?

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

The field lacks a shared-regime boundary between passive/active compliance and feedback-dependent error correction. Apparent disagreement may be entirely caused by unmatched tasks and measurements.

## Established evidence

Quasi-static mechanics establishes that geometry, friction, and compliance affect contact-force paths under restricted assumptions. Physical feedback systems achieve insertion in their individual regimes. A sensorless 0.1-mm result is an abstract-level author claim without reported comparators. [U01; whitney_1982; park_2017; inoue_2017; dong_2021; lenz_2024]

## Relevant explanations

### H1

Within a predictable contact regime, correctly configured compliance accommodates residual error; feedback provides little additional benefit until a mechanical boundary is crossed.

### H2

Feedback materially improves alignment, force safety, or recovery throughout much of the tested regime by detecting latent state that compliance does not resolve.

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

## Why current evidence is insufficient

No evidence card contains a factorial, common-platform comparison that isolates compliance and feedback while measuring initial errors, friction, contact loads, terminal seating, and recovery.

## Potential discriminating observation

Under identical mechanics and initial-condition strata, map the incremental effect of feedback after holding compliance fixed, and map the incremental effect of compliance after holding feedback fixed. A boundary-local benefit supports H1; broad feedback benefit or distinct recovery/force effects supports H2.

## Provisional attack

Use one fixtured rigid peg/hole family and factorially vary clearance, translational/angular error, friction, and stiffness. Compare matched compliant control with and without feedback using a shared action envelope. Measure phase-resolved contact mode, force/torque, jam/wedge, seating, damage proxy, time, and recovery, with replicated randomized trials.

## Why now

Modern sensing and compliant-control stacks make component removal practical, while the literature already identifies the required physical dimensions and failure modes.

## Scope

Rigid, fixtured insertions with controlled surface condition. It cannot establish a universal boundary for deformable, unfixtured, or multi-step assembly.

## Negative-result information

If feedback has no incremental value within the tested boundary, it narrows sensing requirements for that regime. If compliance has no reliable benefit after friction/error variation, it limits direct transfer of quasi-static design intuition. Either outcome informs controller and hardware choices.
