# Candidate C003

## Source uncertainty

**Problem-map node:** U03

## Scientific question

Do anticipatory pursuit and explicit directional prediction read out a shared volatility belief, or separate adaptive learners?

## Scientific uncertainty being reduced

Different fitted adaptation rates in implicit and explicit measures may reflect distinct learners, a shared belief with distinct readout noise, or task/session artifacts.

## Established evidence

Both anticipatory pursuit and ratings adapt to hidden changes in binary motion probability, and a specified change-point model described the published data better than stated comparators. Existing modality measurements were separated by session and do not identify a common latent state.

## Relevant explanations

### H1

A common volatility-sensitive belief is updated trial by trial and read out through distinct pursuit and report functions.

### H2

Implicit pursuit and explicit prediction are driven by separate learners with different learning or hazard parameters.

### H3

One or both fitted-rate differences arise from response scale, task demand, or measurement noise rather than belief dynamics.

## Relationship among explanations

PARTIALLY COMPETING

## Why current evidence is insufficient

Separate sessions and different response demands permit model fits with distinct parameters without showing distinct internal states.

## Potential discriminating observation

In a single interleaved volatile-motion protocol, collect pursuit and an intermittent low-interference prediction report under one hidden sequence. Fit shared-state and separate-state models with held-out switch epochs. H1 predicts trial-resolved cross-modal coupling after measurement noise; H2 predicts dissociable adaptation following matched switches; H3 predicts coupling changes with reporting demand rather than latent-state evidence.

## Provisional attack

Use counterbalanced report-present and report-absent trials, preregister a shared-state state-space model and a separate-learner alternative, and test transfer of parameters across modalities and switch timings.

## Why now

High-resolution eye tracking supports trial-level pursuit estimates, while occasional reports can be inserted without assigning all trials to separate sessions.

## Scope

Human predictive pursuit in a binary, head-restrained motion environment. It does not establish volatility mechanisms in visual search or a neural substrate.

## Negative-result information

Failure of a shared state to predict held-out cross-modal data would limit common-belief claims for this paradigm; failure of separate states to improve prediction would limit the need for separate learners.

## Evidence basis

### Problem-map sections

### Paper cards

### Original sources requiring verification

None required for framing; Pasturel et al. is FULL_TEXT and the active-inference source is theory context.

## Unverified assumptions

Intermittent explicit reports leave pursuit dynamics sufficiently comparable to no-report trials, and the shared-state model’s observation functions are identifiable.

## Construct validity and surviving implementation alternatives

- **Scientific construct identified by the proposed discriminator:** Whether implicit and explicit visual predictions are generated from a shared volatility-sensitive belief state.
- **Implementation-level explanations that could produce the same outcome:** Response interference, temporal alignment error, motor pursuit gain, scale compression, attention, and parameter trade-offs in state-space fits.
- **What this experiment would not identify even if successful:** The neural representation of volatility, transfer to active search, or a uniquely Bayesian learning mechanism.
