
# Literature Index

Discovery scope: mechanistic understanding of in-context learning (ICL) in transformer language models. Records are discovery-level and generally abstract-level; they are not full-text reviews. Classification is relevance to the topic, not endorsement.

## Conceptual clusters

- Foundational capability and measurement: Brown et al. (2020); Min et al. (2022); Zhao et al. (2021); Lu et al. (2022); Webson & Pavlick (2022).
- Induction/copying circuits: Olsson et al. (2022); Bietti et al. (2023).
- Implicit optimization / mesa-learning: von Oswald et al. (2023); Akyürek et al. (2023); Mahankali et al. (2023); Ahn et al. (2023); Wang et al. (2023).
- Bayesian, latent-concept, and data-distribution accounts: Xie et al. (2022); Chan et al. (2022); Raventós et al. (2023); Wies et al. (2023).
- Task representations and large-model mechanisms: Todd et al. (2023); Hendel et al. (2023); Li et al. (2023); Bertsch et al. (2024).
- Emergence, limits, and robustness: Singh et al. (2023); Anwar et al. (2024); Goddard et al. (2025).
- Modern open-model causal localization and task execution: Chan et al. (2023); Tao et al. (2024).
- Induction-head variants and task-vector integration: Singh et al. (2024); Ren et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2025).
- Mechanism-sensitive sequence-learning theory: Edelman et al. (2024).

## Competing explanations and evidence tensions

- Induction heads explain copying/repetition and have strongest causal evidence in small models; their sufficiency for broad semantic/task ICL in large pretrained LMs remains unresolved.
- GD/ridge/Newton accounts explain controlled regression and layerwise algorithmic signatures; they may describe realizable or local mechanisms rather than one universal language-model mechanism.
- Bayesian/latent-concept accounts explain prompt inference, priors, order effects, and data mismatch; they can be behaviorally equivalent to learned optimizers on restricted tasks.
- Task-vector and representation accounts propose an intermediate compressed task state; causal extraction/intervention at scale is still thinner than the behavioral evidence.
- Retrieval/similarity can explain much of long-context ICL, limiting claims of cumulative task learning.
- Pretraining diversity, burstiness, and task distributions affect whether ICL emerges, generalizes, persists, or is replaced by in-weights learning.
- A composite explanation is now plausible in which early previous-token structure organizes demonstrations, later induction/semantic heads route or amplify label-relevant information, and distributed hidden states implement task inference plus verbalization; this remains task- and model-dependent rather than universal.

## Search branches and coverage notes

Branches searched: foundational GPT-3 ICL; induction heads/circuit mechanisms; implicit GD/ridge/preconditioned/Newton optimization; Bayesian latent-variable explanations; task vectors and representation changes; pretraining data diversity/burstiness; prompt order, label, feature, and calibration confounds; long-context retrieval; transient emergence; adversarial robustness; recent task-distribution generalization.

Under-covered: cross-seed replication; tokenizer effects specifically in pretrained open LLMs; training-data tracing; interventions that distinguish retrieval, Bayesian updating, and optimization in the same model; multilingual/code/multimodal ICL; negative results on naturalistic tasks; standardized mechanism-sensitive benchmarks. Modern open-model causal localization, semantic induction-head evidence, and an initial induction-head/task-vector bridge are now represented, but their scope is still limited.
