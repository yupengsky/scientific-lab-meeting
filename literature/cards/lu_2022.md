# Fantastically Ordered Prompts and Where to Find Them: Overcoming Few-Shot Prompt Order Sensitivity

## Metadata
**Authors:** Yao Lu, Max Bartolo, Alastair Moore, Sebastian Riedel, Pontus Stenetorp
**Year:** 2022
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08786
**Published venue:** ACL 2022
**DOI:** —
**Publisher / proceedings:** ACL
**Publication status:** PUBLISHED
**Reading status:** FULL_TEXT

## Abstract
Permutation of a few demonstrations can change classification from near state-of-the-art to chance. The effect persists across model sizes, is not tied to one sample subset, and good orders do not transfer across models. An unlabeled probing set and entropy statistics select useful orders, yielding 13% relative improvement across 11 classification tasks.

## Scientific question
How large and systematic is demonstration-order sensitivity, and can useful orders be selected without labeled development data?

## Main claims
- Order sensitivity is broad and substantial.
- Good permutations are model/task specific.
- Entropy-based probing can select performant prompts in a few-shot-compatible setting.

## Key observations
- Across GPT-2/GPT-3 sizes, 24 orders produce wide score ranges (Fig. 1; Sec. 3).
- Order effects remain at large scale and are not explained by a particular subset (Secs. 3–4; Figs. 2–4).
- Entropy metrics on generated unlabeled probing sets correlate with prompt quality and improve results (Secs. 5–6; Tables 2–4).

## Evidence for major claims
Order experiments: Secs. 3–4, Figs. 1–4. Probing-set construction and metrics: Sec. 5. Eleven-task evaluation: Sec. 6, Tables 2–4. Limitations: Sec. 7.

## Observation vs interpretation
### Observed / demonstrated
- Holding examples and template fixed while permuting examples changes measured accuracy.
- Entropy-based ranking selects orders with higher average accuracy on the evaluated tasks.
### Author interpretation
- Recency, label distribution, and model-specific prompt interactions contribute to order sensitivity; probing entropy is a practical proxy for order quality.
### Inference
- The intervention establishes order as a causal determinant of benchmark output under fixed examples, while the internal cause remains underdetermined.

## Important controls
- Same examples under many permutations; multiple GPT-family sizes; comparisons across tasks and models; labeled development-set oracle versus unlabeled probing method.

## Critical assumptions
- Generated probing examples approximate task inputs and entropy reflects useful prompt behavior.
- The selected order can be evaluated without leaking labels.

## Remaining alternative explanations
- Label-token priors, recency effects, template interactions, and calibration can explain part of the order effect; entropy may exploit dataset-specific regularities.

## Limitations
The authors note computational cost of searching permutations, task/model dependence, and imperfect transfer of selected orders (Sec. 7).

## What this paper supports
Demonstration order is a strong, experimentally manipulable source of ICL variance; unlabeled prompt selection can reduce it in tested classification settings.

## What this paper does NOT establish
It does not identify a unique transformer mechanism, guarantee transfer across models/tasks, or show order selection works for open-ended generation.

## Explicit open questions
Why some orders are good, how order interacts with content/labels, and how to select prompts with lower search cost (Sec. 7).

## Evidence concerns
**Replication:** GPT-3 access limits exact replication.
**Measurement limitations:** Entropy is a proxy, not direct task accuracy.
**Potential confounding:** Order changes recency and label-position statistics jointly.
**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Results focus on text classification and finite candidate permutations.
