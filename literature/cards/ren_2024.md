## Abstract

The paper analyzes attention heads in open LLMs for syntactic dependencies and knowledge-graph relations. It identifies semantic induction heads that attend to subject/head tokens, recall associated object/tail tokens, and increase their output logits. Formation of these heads correlates with emergence of ICL.

## Scientific question

Do open LLM attention heads encode semantic relations in a retrieval-and-logit-increase pattern analogous to induction heads, and does this relate to ICL emergence?

## Main claims

- Some heads implement semantic induction patterns for syntactic and knowledge relations.
- These heads recall relation-associated tokens and increase their logits.
- Their formation is closely correlated with ICL emergence.

## Key observations

- Heads attending to subject/head tokens preferentially retrieve object/tail tokens.
- Output logits for retrieved relation-associated tokens increase.
- Semantic induction-head measures track ICL emergence across analyzed models/conditions.

## Evidence for major claims

- Attention-pattern analysis tests subject-to-object and head-to-tail retrieval (paper sections on syntactic dependency and knowledge graph relations).
- Logit attribution/output analysis tests whether attended tokens are promoted (same analyses).
- Cross-model/training comparisons assess correlation with ICL emergence; this is correlational evidence.

## Observation vs interpretation

### Observed / demonstrated

- Attention heads show relation-specific attention and output-logit effects.
- The measured semantic-head pattern co-occurs with ICL emergence.

### Author interpretation

- The heads encode semantic induction operations and offer a mechanism contributing to ICL.

## Important controls

- Separate syntactic-dependency and knowledge-graph relation settings.
- Analyze attention and output logits jointly rather than attention alone.
- Compare head formation with ICL emergence.

## Critical assumptions

- Attention to a subject/head followed by object/tail logit increase indicates a semantic induction operation.
- Correlation across model development is informative about contribution to ICL.

## Remaining alternative explanations

- Heads may retrieve pretrained semantic knowledge without causing ICL.
- Logit increases may be mediated by downstream circuitry or reflect token frequency/position effects.
- Attribution patterns do not identify a complete causal circuit.

## Limitations

- The central formation–ICL relationship is correlation, not a complete causal intervention.
- The evidence is focused on selected open LLMs and relation tasks.

## What this paper supports

- Induction-like match-and-recall behavior can be semantic and relation-specific in open LLMs.
- Semantic head analyses complement literal token-copying accounts.

## What this paper does NOT establish

- That semantic induction heads are necessary or sufficient for ICL.
- That the identified heads form a complete causal mechanism for knowledge or syntax use.

## Explicit open questions

- Whether ablating or steering these heads changes ICL causally.
- How semantic induction heads interact with MLPs, task vectors, and other attention heads.

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** Published ACL paper; broader model and task replication remains relevant.

**Measurement limitations:** Attention/logit attribution is indirect evidence of computation.

**Potential confounding:** Pretrained knowledge, token frequency, and positional structure may produce similar patterns.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Formation–ICL association is correlational.
