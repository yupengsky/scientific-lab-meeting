# Inference and Verbalization Functions During In-Context Learning

## Metadata
**Authors:** Junyi Tao, Xiaoyin Chen, Nelson F. Liu
**Year:** 2024
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09349
**Published venue:** PREPRINT
**DOI:** UNCLEAR
**Publisher / proceedings:** arXiv
**Publication status:** PREPRINT
**Reading status:** PARTIAL

## Abstract
The paper separates ICL with arbitrary labels into an inference function that solves the task and a verbalization function that maps the answer into the label vocabulary.

## Scientific question
How can models solve a task when demonstration labels are irrelevant or remapped?

## Main claims
- **AUTHOR CLAIM:** Inference is invariant to label remapping, while verbalization maps inferred answers to labels.
- **AUTHOR CLAIM:** The two functions can be localized to different layers.

## Key observations
- Layer-wise interchange interventions support the two-function account across NLI, sentiment, and topic classification, using Gemma-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Gemma-2-27B, and Llama-3.1-70B.

## Evidence for major claims
- **OBSERVATION:** Interchanging selected internal activations across remapped-label conditions preserves task solution or label output in the predicted layer-specific manner.
- **AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** The model computes a label-independent answer before verbalizing it.

## Observation vs interpretation
### Observed / demonstrated
- Controlled layer-wise interchange experiments show separable causal effects for inference and verbalization.
### Author interpretation
- ICL consists of sequential inference and verbalization functions.

## Important controls
- Label remapping; multiple tasks and open models.

## Critical assumptions
- Interchange effects identify functional roles rather than incidental correlated representations.

## Remaining alternative explanations
- Distributed or parallel computation may produce equivalent intervention patterns.

## Limitations
- Evidence is limited to tested classification-style tasks, models, and intervention choices.

## What this paper supports
- Label tokens need not encode the task solution directly; task computation and output coding can be dissociated.

## What this paper does NOT establish
- That all ICL tasks have two serial functions or that layer localization transfers to closed models.

## Explicit open questions
- Whether the decomposition generalizes to generation, reasoning, and multi-step tasks.

## Evidence concerns
**Replication:** Several models/tasks within the paper; external replication not assessed.
**Measurement limitations:** Activation interventions are model- and layer-specific.
**Potential confounding:** Prompt formatting and label-token semantics.
**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Exact effect sizes and intervention robustness require full-text checking.
