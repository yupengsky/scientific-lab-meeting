## Abstract

The paper studies ICL in classification through hidden-state geometry. It defines performance-relevant separability and alignment, reports a two-stage layerwise mechanism, and uses ablations to associate Previous Token Heads with separability and Induction Heads/task vectors with later alignment.

## Scientific question

How do attention heads and task vectors jointly transform hidden states across layers to produce ICL outputs?

## Main claims

- ICL classification proceeds through early query separability followed by later label alignment.
- Previous Token Heads drive early separability.
- Induction Heads and task vectors enhance later alignment.
- Hidden-state geometry provides a unified account linking component-level mechanisms to performance.

## Key observations

- Layerwise separability increases in early layers; alignment increases later.
- Ablating Previous Token Heads reduces separability.
- Ablating or analyzing Induction Heads/task vectors reduces or changes alignment and performance.

## Evidence for major claims

- Layerwise hidden-state measurements establish the separability/alignment trajectory (main experiments).
- Component ablations connect heads and task vectors to the two geometric factors (ablation experiments).
- The evidence is primarily classification-focused and intervention/ablation-based.

## Observation vs interpretation

### Observed / demonstrated

- Hidden-state geometry changes in two layerwise phases in the tested classification tasks.
- Ablations alter the associated geometric factor and model performance.

### Author interpretation

- Previous Token Heads organize demonstrations/query separability, while induction heads and task vectors perform or support label alignment.

## Important controls

- Layerwise analysis separates early and late effects.
- Component ablations test whether geometric changes are linked to specific heads or task vectors.
- Multiple classification experiments and extensive figures/tables are reported.

## Critical assumptions

- Separability and alignment are sufficient summaries of the relevant hidden-state geometry for the tested ICL tasks.
- Ablation effects identify the functional contribution of the targeted components without large compensatory or off-target effects.

## Remaining alternative explanations

- Geometry may summarize behavior without uniquely identifying computation.
- Task vectors and induction heads may be correlated carriers of information rather than separable causal stages.
- Results may depend on classification format and model family.

## Limitations

- Scope is classification ICL; transfer to generation, regression, or naturalistic tasks is unresolved.
- Ablation evidence does not by itself prove a universal architecture of ICL.

## What this paper supports

- A staged composite account connecting early demonstration organization to later label alignment.
- Task-vector and induction-head analyses can be placed in a common hidden-state framework.

## What this paper does NOT establish

- That all ICL uses exactly two stages.
- That task vectors are a single localized object or that induction heads alone perform task inference.

## Explicit open questions

- Whether the geometry and causal roles generalize beyond classification.
- How task vectors are formed and interact with distributed MLP and attention circuits.

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** NeurIPS 2025 paper with extensive experiments; independent cross-model replication remains useful.

**Measurement limitations:** Separability/alignment are constructed geometric measures.

**Potential confounding:** Ablations can change hidden-state geometry indirectly and may interact with prompt format.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Component attribution is ablation-based; necessity, sufficiency, and uniqueness are distinct claims.
