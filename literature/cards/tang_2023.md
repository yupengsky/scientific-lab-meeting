# IndustReal: Transferring Contact-Rich Assembly Tasks from Simulation to Reality

## Scientific question

Can reinforcement-learning policies for contact-rich robotic assembly be trained entirely in simulation and transferred, without real-world policy adaptation, to a real system that performs detection, grasping, placement, and insertion under pose error?

## Exact system and regime

Simulation used Factory in NVIDIA Isaac Gym and PPO policies with task-space impedance control. Real experiments used a Franka Emika Panda arm, wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera, optical breadboard, and industrial-style parts. Tasks: 8/12/16-mm round and rectangular pegs into 0.5-0.6-mm-clearance holes; 20/40/60-mm gears onto 0.5-mm diametral-clearance shafts; and unseen 2- and 3-prong NEMA connectors into receptacles. The system used no F/T sensor, task-specific gripper, demonstrations, baseline plans, dynamics randomization, or real-world policy adaptation (authors' scope statement).

## Main claims

### Simulation-side training algorithms improve insertion-policy learning

**OBSERVATION:** In simulated peg/hole insertions, ablations over 5 seeds x 1,000 trials found Filter-and-Weight SAPU achieved 84.9-87.6% success, while the other SAPU variants did not exceed 40%. For reward ablations, SDF-query distance achieved 88.6 +/- 2.41% success versus 1.8-54.2% for keypoint or Chamfer rewards. Sampling-based curriculum achieved 88.6% success and 3.80-mm position error; other curricula reached at most 66.8% and 10.7 mm.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** SAPU reduces exploitation of inaccurate simulated contact; SDF rewards provide an alignment signal for asymmetric and symmetric objects; SBC prevents overfitting to partially inserted states.

**INFERENCE:** These components are supported as useful within the specified Factory/Isaac Gym peg/hole training setting. The ablations do not isolate their effects in real hardware or establish benefit for substantially different simulators and assembly geometries.

**Intervention or measurement:** Comparisons of SAPU variants, reward formulations, and curricula for simulated Insert policies; success, engagement, terminal pose error, and interpenetration assessed.

**Observed result:** See values above; joint evaluation produced 88.6% peg/hole and 82.0% gear/gearshaft insertion success under moderate randomization and +/-1-mm observation noise.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** No-SAPU, Filter Only, Weight Only; collinear/6-DOF keypoint and Chamfer rewards; no-curriculum and standard-curriculum comparators; five random seeds and 1,000 trials per seed for the stated component evaluations.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** The methods may interact with the selected PPO implementation, simulator physics, CAD assets, reward scaling, and training hyperparameters; the paper evaluates each algorithm while using the other two algorithms.

**Scope limitations:** Evaluated primarily on NIST-inspired peg/hole and gear/shaft insertions in Factory; simulated success may still depend on unmodeled contact phenomena.

**Source pointer:** Sections IV-E-IV-H; Fig. 3-4; Tables I-II and X: https://arxiv.org/html/2305.17110v1

### Deployment-time action integration reduces target error

**OBSERVATION:** On a Reach-policy test, PLAI had approximately 2-mm steady-state error across stated simulated friction/gravity perturbations and real-world dynamics, lower error/variance than nominal action application and lower error than the reported PID comparator. The non-RL Franka task-space-impedance controller yielded 4.45-mm error in the real test; each method used 3 goals x 20 trials.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Integrating policy actions into the prior desired state can compensate deployment-time steady-state error from unmodeled dynamics, while Leaky PLAI limits contact-induced windup.

**INFERENCE:** PLAI is an empirically supported deployment modification for this Franka/TSI/Reach-policy regime; it is not evidence of general disturbance rejection for all learned policies or contact tasks.

**Intervention or measurement:** Nominal, PID (with anti-windup), and PLAI action application for a policy trained in ideal simulation; randomized initial/target poses; steady-state position error measured under ideal, friction, and gravity conditions and in hardware.

**Observed result:** PLAI approximately maintained 2-mm error; it was reported lower than both nominal under disturbances and PID in the test configuration.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** Nominal policy deployment, PID with classical anti-windup, ideal/friction/gravity simulated conditions, and a real-world no-PLAI comparison.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Results are tied to the specific reach policy, disturbance magnitudes, TSI controller, gains, and Franka hardware; error metrics do not fully measure insertion reliability or safety.

**Scope limitations:** Authors acknowledge potential windup during environmental contact; contact mitigation used a leaky variant.

**Source pointer:** Section V-C-V-D; Figs. 5-6; Table IX: https://arxiv.org/html/2305.17110v1

### Simulation-trained policies transfer to the reported real-world assembly system

**OBSERVATION:** Across real trials, Pick succeeded in 158/160 trials (98.75%). Insert succeeded in 100/120 trials (83.33%) with 110/120 engagement (91.67%); full-insertion success by category was 76.7% pegs, 92.5% gears, and 85% connectors. End-to-end PPI succeeded in 107/120 trials (89.16%) with 113/120 engagement (94.17%). Connector insertion used unseen NEMA connectors, and target observations in the Insert experiment had uniform X/Y noise in [-2, 2] mm.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** The system demonstrates repeatable sim-to-real transfer for end-to-end contact-rich assembly, including generalization of the peg/hole Insert policy to NEMA connectors, without real-world policy adaptation.

**INFERENCE:** The evidence supports end-to-end transfer for the reported robot, workcell, parts, fixtures, and trial distributions. It does not establish broad industrial robustness, transfer to unmodeled geometries, or reliability under uncontrolled perception and fixturing conditions.

**Intervention or measurement:** Deploy simulation-trained Pick, Place, and Insert policies plus PLAI on physical pegs, gears, and connectors; measure Pick, partial-insertion engagement, and full-insertion success.

**Observed result:** Reported counts and rates above; authors attribute most engagement failures to gripper-object slip and most full-insertion failures to wedging.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** Real results include multiple assets and separate Pick, Insert, and PPI conditions; no real-world adaptation phase. The paper does not report a hardware baseline comparing transfer methods for the full assembly task.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Success may depend on the manually specified nominal target heights, calibrated workspace, bolted sockets, selected part set, and randomized conditions; qualitative recovery/search observations were not quantified.

**Scope limitations:** The experiments use NIST-inspired tasks and fixed Franka/camera/breadboard hardware; failure modes include slip and wedging.

**Source pointer:** Sections III-A, VI-A-VI-E; Table III; Fig. 7; Appendix B: https://arxiv.org/html/2305.17110v1

## Important controls

- Simulation ablations compare named SAPU, dense-reward, and curriculum alternatives with five seeds and 1,000 trials per seed.
- PLAI comparison includes nominal action application and PID with anti-windup; real PLAI testing uses three goals and 20 trials each.
- Hardware results report separate Pick, Insert, and PPI conditions and per-asset counts (Table III).

## Critical assumptions

- Factory/Isaac Gym contact dynamics plus CAD representations are sufficiently accurate for the target tasks.
- Pose calibration and RGB object detection provide targets within the policy's robustness range.
- Fixed sockets/fixturing, task-space impedance control, and selected randomizations represent the intended operating regime.
- Simulated reward and success definitions correspond to meaningful physical insertion outcomes.

## Limitations

Authors explicitly state that the study primarily investigates NIST-inspired tasks; main real-system failures were gripper slip and wedging; passive mechanical compliance was not explored; and the framework relies on a high-accuracy simulator plus the proposed algorithms. They state that more complex tasks, such as elastic cable assembly onto pulleys, may be neither accurately nor efficiently simulable (Section VIII).

## What this paper supports

- OBSERVATION: In the stated simulation, the three proposed training components improved reported insert-policy metrics relative to their tested alternatives.
- OBSERVATION: In the stated real system, simulation-trained policies achieved the reported Pick, Insert, and PPI trial outcomes without a real-world policy-adaptation phase.
- AUTHOR CLAIM: IndustReal provides a reproducible hardware/software platform for this workflow.

## What this paper does not establish

- That its transfer method works across industrial robots, sensors, controllers, production parts, or unstructured workcells.
- That the three training algorithms independently cause the hardware insertion outcomes.
- Superior real-world end-to-end performance relative to force/torque sensing, demonstrations, classical controllers, domain randomization, or adaptation-based transfer.
- Elimination of contact failures: slip and wedging remain prominent observed failure modes.

## Explicit open questions

- Generalization across much more diverse assembly categories.
- Whether visuotactile sensing and more accurate friction simulation address slip/wedging.
- Joint optimization of policy, controller, and passive mechanical compliance.
- Feasible sim-to-real-to-sim feedback for tasks whose dynamics cannot be simulated accurately or efficiently.

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** The authors provide IndustRealKit CAD/purchasing information and IndustRealLib deployment/training code, and report use on two Franka robots in two cities. Independent replication was not assessed here.

**Measurement limitations:** Reported task outcomes are success/engagement and some pose-error measures; long-run reliability, contact forces, cycle time, wear, and safety margins are not established by the reported experiments.

**Potential confounding:** Calibration correction, manual specification of nominal target heights, bolted sockets, selected assets, and tuned simulation/controller parameters may contribute to results.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Simulation component ablations report five seeds and 1,000 trials per seed, whereas hardware insert/PPI outcomes are mainly 10 trials per asset; no full-task hardware baseline is reported.
