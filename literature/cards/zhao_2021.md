# Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-Shot Performance of Language Models

## Metadata
**Authors:** Tony Z. Zhao, Eric Wallace, Shi Feng, Dan Klein, Sameer Singh
**Year:** 2021
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690
**Published venue:** ICML 2021
**DOI:** —
**Publisher / proceedings:** PMLR
**Publication status:** PUBLISHED
**Reading status:** FULL_TEXT

## Abstract
Few-shot accuracy varies with prompt format, examples, and order. The authors identify majority-label, recency, and common-token biases and estimate a prompt-conditioned output bias using a content-free input. Contextual calibration rescales predictions toward a uniform content-free distribution, improving average accuracy by up to 30 points and reducing prompt variance.

## Scientific question
Can prompt-induced output biases explain few-shot instability, and can measuring them improve performance without labeled development data?

## Main claims
- Prompt choice can move accuracy from near chance to near state of the art.
- Output-distribution biases are a major source of this instability.
- Contextual calibration improves accuracy and robustness across GPT-2/GPT-3 tasks.

## Key observations
- Changing example order, format, or examples changes accuracy substantially (Sec. 3; Fig. 1; Tables 1–2).
- Content-free inputs reveal unequal class/token probabilities associated with prompt bias (Sec. 4; Fig. 2).
- Calibration improves mean accuracy and lowers standard deviation across prompts on classification and multiple-choice tasks (Sec. 5; Figs. 1, 3–5; Tables 3–6).

## Evidence for major claims
Instability: Sec. 3. Bias diagnostics: Sec. 4. Method and equations: Sec. 4.1. Results/ablations: Sec. 5, Tables 3–6. Limitations: Sec. 6.

## Observation vs interpretation
### Observed / demonstrated
- The model’s prediction distribution on “N/A” or other content-free inputs is non-uniform and changes with the prompt.
- Applying the estimated affine calibration changes task scores and prompt variance.
### Author interpretation
- The non-uniform content-free distribution reflects contextual/label biases that distort task predictions.
### Inference
- Calibration demonstrates that output-space normalization is a useful intervention; it does not prove every accuracy failure originates from the measured bias.

## Important controls
- GPT-2 and GPT-3 scales; multiple datasets and prompt choices; uncalibrated baseline; alternative content-free inputs and calibration variants; random prompt-order analyses.

## Critical assumptions
- A content-free input elicits prompt bias without task-relevant evidence.
- A uniform target distribution is appropriate for the answer space.

## Remaining alternative explanations
- Calibration may help through changed verbalizer priors or decision thresholds, independent of the proposed causal account; task-label imbalance and prompt semantics may remain.

## Limitations
The method depends on a known finite answer set and is most direct for classification/multiple-choice; calibration inputs and label verbalizers can affect estimates (Secs. 4–6).

## What this paper supports
Prompt-conditioned output bias is measurable and correcting it can improve few-shot classification reliability.

## What this paper does NOT establish
It does not provide a mechanistic account inside the transformer, guarantee gains on generation/open-ended tasks, or show calibration removes all prompt sensitivity.

## Explicit open questions
How to calibrate broader task types and understand the source of the biases across models and prompts (Sec. 6).

## Evidence concerns
**Replication:** GPT-3 access limits exact replication.
**Measurement limitations:** Content-free probes are operational definitions of bias.
**Potential confounding:** Calibration changes class priors and can compensate for unrelated errors.
**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Gains vary by task, model, prompt, and verbalizer.
