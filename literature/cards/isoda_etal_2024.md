# Dynamic spatial representation of self and others’ actions in the macaque frontal cortex

## Metadata

**Authors:** Taihei Ninomiya; Masaki Isoda

**Year:** 2024

**arXiv:** N/A

**Published venue:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America 121(31), e2403445121

**DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.2403445121

**Publication status:** PUBLISHED

**READING_STATUS:** FULL_TEXT

**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11295024/

**Evidence role:** CORE

## Scientific question

How are the spatial locations of self-generated and partner-generated actions represented by single neurons in macaque ventral premotor cortex (PMv) and medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC), and does a partner’s social relevance alter that representation?

## Exact system and regime

Two Japanese macaques (Macaca fuscata) performed a role-reversal, three-target reaching task while facing a live monkey (real-agent, RA), a life-sized prerecorded monkey video (filmed-monkey, FM), or a prerecorded moving wooden stick (filmed-object, FO). The two monkeys alternated actor/observer roles every three trials. Multicontact probes recorded left-hemisphere PMv and MPFC single neurons; spatial tuning was evaluated during −400 to +200 ms around target-button press.

## Main claims

### Both PMv and MPFC contain agent-related neurons whose firing is spatially selective for self and/or partner actions in the live-partner condition.

**OBSERVATION:** In the RA condition, 124/301 agent-related PMv neurons (41%) and 79/226 agent-related MPFC neurons (35%) showed significant spatial preference; the area difference was nonsignificant (P = 0.15). Individual cells could prefer ipsilateral or contralateral action locations.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** PMv and MPFC both encode spatial information about self and other actions.

**INFERENCE:** The recordings support a population code correlated with action location; they do not establish that either region is required for spatial action judgments.

**Intervention or measurement:** Single-unit firing in peri-action windows; Spearman rank tests on left/center/right target positions, checked with one-way ANOVA plus Tukey–Kramer tests.

**Observed result:** Comparable fractions of agent-related neurons were spatially tuned in PMv and MPFC; ipsilateral and contralateral counts did not differ significantly within either area.

**Causal strength:** CORRELATIONAL

**Controls:** Correct action trials were used by agent-preference class; an independent nonmonotonic ANOVA test yielded comparable tuning conclusions; partner-error comparisons tested the effect of outcome/correctness.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Uncontrolled gaze and body/eye coordinate frames can affect apparent location tuning; reward expectation may contribute, particularly in PMv.

**Scope limitations:** Two animals, left-hemisphere recordings, reach-to-target actions, and a constrained face-to-face task.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Neurons Exhibiting Spatial Preference for Self-Action and Other-Action,” Fig. 2, Table 1, SI Appendix Table S1; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11295024/

### MPFC spatial tuning, unlike PMv tuning, was reduced when the partner was filmed rather than live.

**OBSERVATION:** The fraction of spatially tuned MPFC neurons declined for partner action from 24% (RA) to 12% (FM) and 11% (FO), and for self action from 22% (RA) to 16% (FM) and 9% (FO). Corresponding PMv proportions did not significantly differ across partner conditions. MPFC tuning-strength distributions were broader with the live partner.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** MPFC spatial coding is sensitive to social relevance and PMv coding reflects movement more independently of partner social relevance.

**INFERENCE:** The manipulation associates live social context with MPFC tuning; it cannot isolate live interaction from co-location, olfaction, or other sensory/contextual differences.

**Intervention or measurement:** Partner condition varied between a live monkey, matched-appearance prerecorded monkey, and prerecorded object; single-unit spatial tuning and slope distributions compared across conditions.

**Observed result:** RA-versus-FM and RA-versus-FO reductions in MPFC partner-action tuning were significant (P = 2.7×10⁻³ and 2.4×10⁻³); RA-versus-FO self-action reduction was significant (P = 1.6×10⁻³).

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** FM matched RA partner appearance/size and body-coordinate layout; task sounds were generated similarly across conditions; response-time split analyses found no significant tuning difference; gaze duration at correct targets increased, rather than decreased, as partner social relevance decreased.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** RA uniquely includes monkey odor, live contingent interaction, and shared physical space; attention/arousal and unmeasured sensory signals remain incompletely controlled.

**Scope limitations:** FM and FO combine several features, so “social relevance” is a composite manipulation; effects are based on a two-monkey sample.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Effect of the Partner’s Social Relevance on Spatial Preference,” Fig. 3, Table 2, SI Appendix Figs. S1–S3; Discussion, paragraph beginning “It is not clear exactly…”.

### A minority of dual-tuned neurons reversed spatial preference between self and partner actions, consistent with actor-centered coding.

**OBSERVATION:** In RA, 27 PMv and 24 MPFC neurons had spatial preference for both self and partner actions. Of these, 8 PMv and 9 MPFC neurons had opposite (incongruent) preference directions; congruent cells numbered 19 and 15. Incongruent cell counts generally exceeded bootstrap null expectations.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Incongruent responses are consistent with an actor-centered coordinate transformation and may provide a substrate for spatial perspective-taking.

**INFERENCE:** Reversed tuning demonstrates a coding pattern compatible with actor-centered coordinates; it does not show that monkeys used perspective-taking, because that operation was unnecessary for the task.

**Intervention or measurement:** Self and partner action-location tuning compared within individual neurons and pseudopopulation decoding; gaze behavior and bootstrap null distributions evaluated.

**Observed result:** Incongruent cells changed firing monotonically in opposite directions for self versus partner actions; selected PMv and MPFC pseudopopulations decoded partner-action locations above permutation nulls.

**Causal strength:** CORRELATIONAL

**Controls:** Many incongruent cells were co-recorded with congruent cells; target gaze durations did not differ across sessions that yielded incongruent versus congruent cells; bootstrap analyses addressed chance occurrence.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Eye/body coordinate frame is unresolved because fixation was not imposed; small number of dual/incongruent cells and pseudopopulations assembled across sessions limit conclusions about network-level computation.

**Scope limitations:** Spatial perspective-taking was not behaviorally required; no PMv/MPFC perturbation was performed.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Incongruent Spatial Preference Between Self-Action and Partner-Action,” Fig. 4, Table 2, SI Appendix Figs. S4–S7; Discussion, paragraphs beginning “A subset…” and “The aforementioned possibilities…”.

## Important controls

- FM preserved the monkey’s apparent body and size while removing live interaction; FO supplied a nonbiological comparison.
- Behavioral performance remained high across conditions; response-time and gaze analyses addressed kinematic and reduced-attention accounts.
- Spatial tuning was tested both with monotonic Spearman analysis and an ANOVA/post hoc alternative.
- Error trials and simultaneous congruent/incongruent recordings helped assess reward/correctness and session-level gaze explanations.
- Permutation testing evaluated pseudopopulation decoding; bootstrap testing evaluated counts of dual-tuned cell types.

## Critical assumptions

- RA, FM, and FO conditions rank social relevance as intended.
- Peri-action firing differences primarily reflect spatial action coding rather than unmeasured eye position, sensory differences, reward expectation, or movement kinematics.
- Pseudopopulations formed across sessions approximate the relevant population information.
- Spearman slope sign adequately captures ipsilateral/contralateral spatial preference.

## Limitations

- Authors explicitly state that free gaze leaves the neural coordinate frame (eye- versus body-centered) unresolved.
- The task did not require spatial perspective-taking, so the proposed link from incongruent coding to this ability remains tentative.
- The live-partner manipulation confounds candidate sensory factors (including olfaction) with real-time interaction and shared space.
- No inactivation or stimulation tests whether PMv or MPFC is causal for perspective-taking or social action processing.
- Data come from two macaques and single-neuron samples across sessions.

## What this paper supports

- In this task, single-neuron spatial tuning for self and partner reaches exists in both PMv and MPFC.
- A live versus filmed partner context changes the prevalence and strength of MPFC spatial tuning, while the reported PMv proportions remain stable.
- Some neurons exhibit self/other tuning reversals compatible with actor-centered spatial coding.

## What this paper does not establish

- That PMv or MPFC causally implements spatial perspective-taking, imitation, or theory of mind.
- The exact component of live social interaction responsible for MPFC changes.
- A defined eye-, body-, or actor-centered reference frame.
- Generality to other actions, social partners, hemispheres, species, or human clinical conditions.

## Explicit open questions

- Which sensory or contextual factor determines the MPFC effect of social relevance.
- Whether tuning changes with angular disparity between self and other viewpoints.
- Whether PMv or MPFC inactivation impairs attributing visual perception to another agent.
- How signals from hands, gaze, head, and other body parts are integrated for higher-level social cognition.

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** Two subjects; no separate replication cohort reported.

**Measurement limitations:** Extracellular single-unit activity cannot determine synaptic/circuit mechanism; recording only from left PMv/MPFC and free viewing prevent reference-frame identification.

**Potential confounding:** Live condition differs from filmed conditions in social contingency, shared real space, smell, and potentially other sensory and attentional features.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Incongruent neuron subgroups are small; pseudopopulation decoding pools neurons recorded in different sessions; several analyses use multiple categorical comparisons.
