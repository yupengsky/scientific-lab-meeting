# Effort drives saccade selection

## Metadata

**Authors:** Damian Koevoet; Christoph Strauch

**Year:** 2025

**arXiv:** None identified

**Published venue:** eLife 13:RP97760

**DOI:** 10.7554/eLife.97760

**Publication status:** PUBLISHED

**READING_STATUS:** FULL_TEXT

**Source URL:** https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97760

**Evidence role:** CORE

## Scientific question

Do direction-specific intrinsic saccade costs, indexed by task-evoked pupil dilation, predict which saccade people choose in controlled choice and natural-scene search; and does increased cognitive demand preferentially reduce costly saccades?

## Exact system and regime

Twenty adults completed two 360-trial sessions: plan/withhold and then execute 10° saccades in 36 directions, followed by free choice between two direction targets. Pupil size and gaze were recorded at 1 kHz in a luminance-controlled, head-restrained laboratory. Secondary analyses used two prior natural-scene letter-search datasets (n=16 and n=25); experiment 2 included an auditory digit-counting dual task. (Methods; Figs. 1–4.)

## Main claims

### Direction-specific pupillary cost maps predict controlled saccade choice

**OBSERVATION:** In the planning task, pupil dilation was greater for oblique than cardinal directions and for downward than upward saccades, after modeled saccade-property covariates. The same participants preferred cardinal and upward directions in binary choice. Direction-level planning pupil size correlated negatively with preference (r(34)=-0.76, p<0.001); the lower-pupil option was selected 56.64% on average (95% CI 52.75–60.52%).

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Pupil dilation measures intrinsic saccade effort/cost, and participants preferentially select affordable saccades.

**INFERENCE:** Direction-specific planning-related pupil differences are predictive of choice under this task design; their physiological source and whether they are solely “cost” are unresolved.

**Intervention or measurement:** Cued saccade direction during planning; subsequent unconstrained binary target selection; 1-kHz pupil size and eye position.

**Observed result:** Cost-map/preference correspondence and above-chance selection of the lower-pupil option.

**Causal strength:** CORRELATIONAL

**Controls:** Equiluminant targets calibrated per participant; same amplitude; direction effects modeled with duration, amplitude, landing error and peak velocity; a control analysis addressed latency and landing precision; baseline correction and pre-movement pupil window.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Pupil dilation reflects multiple sensory/cognitive processes; planning and immediate choice may recruit partially different oculomotor programs.

**Scope limitations:** 20 young adults; 10° saccades, 36 directions, simplified screen task; effects quantify direction bias rather than all determinants of gaze choice.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Saccade costs differ…” and “Saccade costs predict…,” Figs. 1–2; Methods, “Saccade planning and saccade preference tasks.”

### Cost difference covaries with conflict markers during choice

**OBSERVATION:** Larger between-option pupil-cost differences were associated with greater curvature away from the unchosen option and longer latency. The cost map predicted selection for away-curving (59.72%) but not toward-curving trials.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Cost is actively weighed during selection, particularly when its difference warrants a more elaborate decision.

**INFERENCE:** The associations are compatible with cost-sensitive competition; curvature and latency alone do not identify the computation causing them.

**Intervention or measurement:** Cost difference derived from planning map; choice-saccade curvature and onset latency.

**Observed result:** Curvature and latency increased with cost difference (both p<0.001); selection-prediction accuracies differed by curvature direction (p<0.001).

**Causal strength:** CORRELATIONAL

**Controls:** Exclusions for very short/long latency or duration, small amplitude, and target separation >150°; within-participant mixed models.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Choice difficulty, spatial competition, or unmeasured target/motor factors can co-vary with curvature/latency.

**Scope limitations:** Derived from the controlled binary task and an aggregate pupil cost map.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Saccade costs predict saccade curvature and latency,” Fig. 3; Methods, “Data processing and analyses.”

### Natural-scene search and dual task are consistent with resource-sensitive cost avoidance

**OBSERVATION:** In two natural-scene search datasets, pupil size was associated with direction preference after covariate adjustment. Under an auditory-counting dual task, participants made fewer saccades (t(24)=7.224, p<0.001) and the direction-adjustment map related to the single-task pupil map (p<0.001), with costly directions reduced disproportionately.

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Saccade cost remains a driver of choice in natural viewing; cognitive and oculomotor operations draw partly on shared resources that are flexibly allocated.

**INFERENCE:** Increasing task demand caused a change in saccade frequency and direction distribution in this priority-instructed paradigm; a shared resource pool is a plausible explanation, not directly measured.

**Intervention or measurement:** Reanalysis of two visual-search datasets; auditory digit count versus ignore instruction in experiment 2; pupil, gaze direction and covariates including luminance, saliency, fixation/trial number, gaze position and saccade properties.

**Observed result:** Negative cost-preference relations in each dataset; dual task reduced overall and high-pupil-cost directions.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** Mixed models adjust listed pupil covariates; task instructed participants to prioritize auditory counting; separate within-dataset direction maps; data-quality exclusions.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Dual-task effects can reflect strategic task prioritization, search-performance changes, residual luminance/salience confounding, or floor effects for already rare oblique saccades.

**Scope limitations:** Natural-scene datasets were previously collected; no direct manipulation of saccade cost; auditory task was explicitly primary.

**Source pointer:** Results, “Saccade costs predict… natural viewing,” Fig. 4; Methods, “Search in natural scenes.”

## Important controls

- Participant-specific flicker-fusion equiluminance and a constant brightness-controlled display (Methods, Apparatus and stimuli).
- Fixed amplitude and cued direction in the cost measure; pre-saccade pupil window avoids pupil-foreshortening contamination (Methods).
- Natural-viewing models included luminance, saliency, timing, gaze, and saccade covariates (Methods).
- The authors explicitly label the map/choice evidence correlational and the dual-task result causal (Discussion).

## Critical assumptions

- Task-evoked pupil dilation before movement validly indexes direction-specific effort/cost.
- The planning cost map transfers to the preference task.
- Regression covariates sufficiently reduce non-effort influences on pupil size in natural viewing.

## Limitations

Authors state pupil size is sluggish and affected by many sensory and cognitive factors; pupil size alone cannot identify the origin of cost or related anisotropies. They state that the overlapping resources between counting and oculomotor tasks, cost components, and neural circuitry remain open. (Discussion.)

## What this paper supports

Direction-dependent, planning-related pupil dilation predicts direction choice in a controlled task; this relation also appears in covariate-adjusted natural-scene search. Increasing cognitive demand changed the distribution and number of saccades in the dual-task dataset.

## What this paper does not establish

It does not establish that pupil dilation is a specific or exhaustive measure of saccade cost, identify a neural mechanism, prove that shared working memory explains the dual-task effect, or show cost dominates salience, goals, or selection history.

## Explicit open questions

- Which processes create saccade costs and how they shape different saccade aspects.
- How cost relates to perceptual/attentional/oculomotor anisotropies.
- Which cognitive resources overlap across oculomotor and other tasks, and the relevant neural circuitry. (Discussion.)

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** Controlled cost-map result builds on the authors’ earlier work; no independent replication included.

**Measurement limitations:** Pupil dilation is a non-specific, temporally sluggish physiological marker; natural viewing has numerous covariates.

**Potential confounding:** Cross-task correspondence, residual luminance/salience effects, choice difficulty, and instructed dual-task priority remain relevant.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Controlled sample n=20; natural-scene analyses are secondary analyses of existing datasets; causal manipulation changes cognitive demand rather than saccade cost directly.
