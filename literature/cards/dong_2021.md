# Tactile-RL for Insertion: Generalization to Objects of Unknown Geometry

## Metadata

**Authors:** Siyuan Dong; Devesh K. Jha; Diego Romeres; Sangwoon Kim; Daniel Nikovski; Alberto Rodriguez

**Year:** 2021

**arXiv:** 2104.01167

**Published venue:** 2021 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), pp. 6437-6443

**DOI:** 10.1109/ICRA48506.2021.9561646

**Publication status:** PUBLISHED

**READING_STATUS:** FULL_TEXT

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.01167 (full text inspected); https://www.merl.com/publications/TR2021-052.html

**Evidence role:** HIGH-LEVERAGE SUPPORTING

## Scientific question

Can a real-robot, tactile-feedback RL policy correct residual object-hole pose error and insert objects of previously unseen geometry without using prior object geometry, and how do RL formulation, curriculum, tactile representation, and F/T sensing affect this result?

## Exact system and regime

6-DoF Mitsubishi Electric RV-4FL arm; WSG-32 parallel-jaw gripper; two GelSlim vision-based tactile sensors. TD3 actor used two 0.5-s tactile sequences (12 downsampled 640x480 images from each sensor); action was continuous gripper-frame (delta-x, delta-y, delta-yaw). Training used four approximately 35-mm-wide objects (cylinder, hexagonal cylinder, elliptical cylinder, cuboid) and a curriculum of line wall, corner, U wall, then hole; U/hole clearance was 3 mm. Tests comprised 250 insertions across four training and four novel everyday objects (big/small bottle, phone charger, paper box), with x/y errors sampled from -5 to 5 mm and yaw from -10 to 10 degrees. Success was inferred when downward motion did not trigger the slip detector. [Sections IV.A-B, V; Fig. 3; Table I]

## Main claims

### Curriculum TD3 with tactile marker-flow input generalizes within this test set

**OBSERVATION:** The RL* policy (TD3 + supervised warm start + curriculum + tactile marker-flow input) achieved 97.1%, 97.1%, 98.0%, and 89.6% success on the four training objects; on novel big bottle, small bottle, phone charger, and paper box it achieved 80.8%, 96.7%, 96.8%, and 70.1%, respectively. Mean attempts among successful trials were 2.10-7.91. Training used about 8 hours and 3,000 data points. [Section V.A; Table I]

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** A tactile RL policy can generalize across object geometries without prior object geometry; the authors characterize the novel-object performance as good, with lower performance for chamfered big bottle and paper box.

**INFERENCE:** Under the reported robot, clearance, pose-error distribution, object set, and success detector, tactile feedback contains sufficient task-relevant information for a policy trained on four shapes to transfer to these four held-out objects. It does not demonstrate arbitrary-geometry generalization.

**Intervention or measurement:** Train the stated RL* configuration on four objects and test insertion success/attempts on four training and four held-out objects.

**Observed result:** See success and attempt values above.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** Same task metrics and pose-error regime across object evaluations; comparisons with supervised, no-curriculum, RGB-input, and F/T RL policies in Table I.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Generalization may depend on visual/tactile similarities among the eight selected objects, fixed gripper/sensors, clearance, reset fixtures, and the prescribed error distribution.

**Scope limitations:** Single hardware setup and insertion geometry family; four held-out objects; maximum 15 attempts; residual error assumes an upstream vision estimate.

**Source pointer:** arXiv full text, Sections IV.A-B and V.A, pp. 3-5; Fig. 3; Table I. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.01167

### The ablation associates curriculum and marker-flow representation with better held-out-object performance

**OBSERVATION:** In Table I, mean success over the four novel objects is about 86% for RL*, versus about 37% for RL without curriculum and about 55% for RL with RGB tactile input. The no-curriculum policy was trained directly in the hole with the same four training objects, tactile marker flow, supervised bootstrap, and total training data as RL*. The RGB policy performed similarly to RL* on training objects but substantially worse on several novel objects. [Section V.B; Table I]

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** Curriculum improves data efficiency and ability to handle hard rotational-error cases; marker flow limits overfitting to object-specific texture/shape features relative to raw RGB.

**INFERENCE:** These experiments support the selected curriculum and representation for this implementation, but their separate causal contributions are not cleanly isolated from training trajectory and other implementation choices.

**Intervention or measurement:** Compare RL* with direct-hole/no-curriculum marker-flow RL and raw-RGB tactile RL using insertion success and attempts.

**Observed result:** No-curriculum RL reached 57.1% cuboid success and about 30% mean novel-object success; RGB RL had 88.0% cuboid success but 37.8% big-bottle and 13.4% paper-box success.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** No-curriculum condition used the same bootstrap, marker-flow input, training objects, and total data as RL*; RGB condition retained the RL setting while changing tactile input representation.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Results may reflect hyperparameter or training-schedule sensitivity; reported experiment count and uncertainty estimates per policy/object are absent.

**Scope limitations:** Comparisons are specific to the chosen TD3 architectures, sensor preprocessing, curriculum order, and object/environment set.

**Source pointer:** arXiv full text, Section V.B, p. 5; Table I. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.01167

### Tactile RL outperforms F/T RL on the selected non-round shapes, while F/T is competitive on round-edged objects

**OBSERVATION:** F/T RL achieved 99.5% and 95.2% success on novel big and small bottles, but 53.5% on phone charger and 54.1% on paper box; RL* achieved 80.8%, 96.7%, 96.8%, and 70.1% on those objects. F/T RL also had 61.2% success on the cuboid versus 89.6% for RL*. [Table I; Section V.B]

**AUTHOR INTERPRETATION:** F/T sensing is effective for x/y contact-error localization but has difficulty distinguishing rotation; tactile sensing also observes object motion during contact, which may provide information for rotation-error differentiation and better geometry generalization.

**INFERENCE:** The reported data support an advantage of this GelSlim-based policy over the particular F/T-policy implementation for several angular/cuboid-like test objects; they do not establish that tactile sensing universally dominates F/T sensing.

**Intervention or measurement:** Train a separate TD3 RL policy from 0.5-s streams of F/T signals (with vertical-axis torque removed) and compare its test performance to tactile RL*.

**Observed result:** F/T was equal or better on several round-edged objects and lower on the selected cuboid-shaped objects.

**Causal strength:** INTERVENTION EVIDENCE

**Controls:** Same physical setup, task, success metric, and test objects; both were RL policies, although their actor architectures differed to fit data format.

**Alternative explanations not excluded:** Sensor modality is confounded with different network architectures, F/T input dimensionality/preprocessing, and training schedule (F/T trained directly in hole environment).

**Scope limitations:** One F/T sensor and one tactile-sensor design; no multimodal policy; no matched architecture or sensor-noise analysis.

**Source pointer:** arXiv full text, Sections IV.E and V.B, pp. 4-5; Table I. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.01167

## Important controls

- Supervised marker-flow policy used the same architecture as the RL actor and the same 15-attempt cap. [Section IV.D]
- No-curriculum RL retained the RL* marker-flow input, supervised bootstrap, training objects, and total training data. [Section V.B]
- RGB versus marker-flow ablation held the policy type constant but changed tactile input representation. [Sections III.D, V.B]
- Test errors were sampled uniformly in the stated x/y/yaw range; results report 250 insertion experiments across the eight objects. [Section V]

## Critical assumptions

- An external vision system supplies a noisy initial hole position; tactile RL corrects the remaining relative pose error. [Section IV.B]
- Absence of a slip-detector trigger during descent denotes successful insertion. [Section IV.B]
- Controlled resetting, grasp forces, and heights reduce overfitting to height or local tactile-image features. [Section IV.B]
- During training, true contact error is available to the critic through controlled grasp/reset procedures; it is not the actor input at deployment. [Section IV.C]

## Limitations

- Authors state failure commonly occurred when translational and yaw errors were both large; weakly captured rotation can lead the policy to act in the wrong direction and diverge. [Section V.A]
- The paper states marker flow may not be the best representation and leaves learning a good representation to future work. [Section V.B]
- The setup has fixed 3-mm clearance, a limited object set, and a 15-attempt cap; broad object/environment/robot transfer was not tested. [Sections IV.A-C, V]

## What this paper supports

Real-robot evidence that, in this controlled insertion regime, a TD3 policy using paired GelSlim tactile sequences, a staged environment curriculum, and marker-flow input attained high success on four selected held-out objects and compared favorably with the paper's supervised, no-curriculum, RGB-input, and F/T baselines on several difficult shapes.

## What this paper does not establish

Universal generalization to unknown geometry; a modality-only causal advantage of tactile over F/T sensing; deployment without an initial visual pose estimate or automatic reset fixtures; or statistical reliability across independent training seeds, robots, sensor instances, and broader object/clearance distributions.

## Explicit open questions

- Learning a tactile representation better than marker flow. [Section V.B]
- How to recover/act on rotational error reliably in difficult, partially observable contact configurations. [Sections V.A-B]

## Evidence concerns

**Replication:** No independent replication, multiple training seeds, or confidence intervals are reported in the inspected paper.

**Measurement limitations:** Success is operationalized through a slip detector during vertical descent; independent geometric verification of final insertion is not described in the result section.

**Potential confounding:** Tactile and F/T policies differ in input representation, actor architecture, and training procedure; modality comparison is therefore not a pure sensor swap.

**Statistical / experimental concerns:** Table I gives percentages and mean attempts but no dispersion, per-object trial count, seed count, or hypothesis tests. It states 250 insertion experiments across the test set, leaving the allocation across conditions unclear.
